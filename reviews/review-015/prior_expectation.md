# Prior expectation (review-015-1)

Written after reading only the title and §1 Introduction (which contains the "Research question" paragraph) of run-015/paper.md at tag submission-1. The abstract and the rest of the paper were not read before this file was saved.

## Research question as I understood it
For fixed portfolios of locations in operational ensemble temperature forecasts, how tight are bounds on P(at least k of n locations exceed a threshold) when only (a) per-location exceedance probabilities, (b) an aggregate second moment (sum of pairwise joint probabilities / E[S^2]) or (c) the full pairwise table is released, and how often do these bounds certify an illustrative binary action (e.g. "act if P >= alpha")? Exploratory: subset queries, city locations, a four-time maximum.

## My expected answer (before reading the results)
1. Marginals only: bounds are the classical Frechet/Boole-type bounds (lower max(0,(sum p - k + 1)/(n - k + 1)), upper min(1, sum p / k) for "at least k"). In real forecast states many locations are near-certain (p = 0 or 1, especially for fixed thresholds over a domain), so a large fraction of cases will be trivially tight or far from the decision threshold; for the remaining cases with several intermediate probabilities the interval will be wide (often tens of percentage points).
2. Aggregate second moment: the discrete moment problem (Prekopa / Boros-Prekopa binomial-moment bounds, Kwerel/Dawson-Sankoff type) narrows the interval noticeably relative to marginals, and gives a large share of the pairwise improvement for k near the mean; less so for extreme k.
3. Full pairwise table: LP (Hailperin) bounds are tighter than the aggregate moment, but for k >= 3 of n >= 4 not exact; some residual width remains. With finite ensembles (50 or 51 members), integrality/known ensemble size and exact zero cells will tighten bounds further, possibly making many cases exact.
4. Certification: a majority of cases (my guess 70-90%) will already be certified by marginals because the event is far from the decision boundary; pairwise will raise certification modestly (a few to ~15 percentage points); aggregate moment will capture part of that gain. The answer will depend strongly on threshold/k choice and on the decision probability alpha.
5. Exploratory extensions: subset queries not specified in advance cannot be reconstructed from a portfolio aggregate moment (needs the pairwise table); four-time maximum will reduce certification because it is a higher-order event in time.

Confidence: moderate (about 60%) on the qualitative ordering marginals < aggregate moment < pairwise in tightness, high (about 85%) that the ordering itself holds (it is implied by information nesting); low (about 30%) on the specific magnitudes I guessed.

## How researchers and practitioners would judge this now (concretely)
- Multivariate forecast post-processing researchers (ensemble copula coupling, Schaake shuffle, Gaussian copula methods) already know that per-location probabilities do not identify joint events and that dependence must be restored from raw ensemble ranks or historical data. They would regard the bounding inequalities (Frechet, Boole-Bonferroni, Hailperin LP, Prekopa discrete moment problem) as textbook and would view the qualitative result (pairwise > aggregate > marginal, bounds wide when probabilities intermediate) as predictable. What would be new to them is only an empirical quantification on operational forecast states and a clear denominator of how often it matters for a decision.
- Ensemble-product designers (e.g. ECMWF, national met services delivering probability maps/histograms) would judge that most downstream users receive per-location probabilities, that raw members are available for those who need joint events, and that delivering pairwise tables is unusual. They would likely judge "if you need joint events, use members or scenarios", without a quantitative sense of how often marginal-only products are decision-insufficient.
- Heat-health practitioners (heat-health action plans) mostly use deterministic or per-location probabilistic thresholds, often with health-relevant indices (apparent temperature, WBGT) and local criteria; a "k of n locations" capacity framing is not standard practice, so they would treat any result on simplified temperature portfolios as illustrative, not as guidance for their trigger.

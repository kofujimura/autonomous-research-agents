# Value to readers (review-018-1)

This is the reviewer's self-report of how the study could change the understanding or judgment of its intended readers (wetland reference-assessment researchers, remote-sensing researchers who build embedding-based screening, and practitioners in agencies/consultancies). It is not a measurement of any actual change in readers' behaviour.

## Comparison with the prior expectation (prior_expectation.md, saved 08:30:33Z)
- As expected: the raw-cosine augmentation yields a small point reduction in inventory-regime mismatch (primary window-mean -2.95 pp AlphaEarth, -3.72 pp TESSERA vs the hydrological pair model), with window-bootstrap intervals reaching or crossing zero, heterogeneity across windows, and a ceiling far from zero (all methods 31–42 % mismatch; uniform random reference 45 %). Geographic transfer did not produce a stable gain. My expectation of "a few points with wide intervals" was met almost exactly.
- Different from expectation: (1) the size of the increment is of the same order as the change produced by re-seeding the forest or adding a constant, random or permuted column (window-mean changes from -4.56 to +3.92 pp for null perturbations), so the matching endpoint at this design cannot separate embedding information from model randomness; I had not anticipated that the random-forest + top-1 + 50 %-screen pipeline would be this unstable. (2) More than half of the AlphaEarth headline gain comes from one window with 5 retained queries (R07: one query changed from mismatch to match = -1.67 of -2.95 pp); on the pooled scale the AlphaEarth gain is -0.67 pp (reviewer's window-bootstrap of pooled counts -2.47 to +1.20), while TESSERA's is -2.44 pp (-5.03 to -0.32); the pooled scale thus suggests a small TESSERA gain and no detectable AlphaEarth gain against the pair model at seed 0. (3) In the recent-image panel, where inventory images are closest in time to the 2024 embeddings, the gain disappeared (AlphaEarth pooled change 0.00 pp), against my expectation that label age would hide rather than create a gain. (4) The probabilistic decoder diagnostic (planned) shows clearer evidence of regime information in the embeddings than the matching endpoint does (reviewer's window-level re-analysis: hybrid AlphaEarth decoder log loss -0.047 nats vs hydrological decoder, 95 % window-bootstrap -0.073 to -0.019, better in 9/12 windows).

## Which understanding or judgment could change
- Method developers / remote-sensing researchers (C03, C04, C06, C05, C10): a favourable before/after comparison of a stochastic learner with and without an embedding feature can be produced by any added column; matched null-feature, seed and permutation controls and a fixed-query decomposition are needed before claiming an increment. Trust: high that the reported numbers are what the archived pipeline produces (re-run and own recomputation agree); the general lesson is largely predictable from how random forests sample features, so the novelty of the lesson is modest, but the quantified size relative to the claimed increment is informative.
- Wetland reference-assessment researchers (C01, C02, C05): on this convenience panel, adding annual embedding distance to explicit terrain/surface-water/HAND covariates does not give a robust improvement in matching NWI water-regime categories; the improvement, if any, is a few points at most and below the noise of the evaluation. This could moderate expectations that embedding similarity search would by itself produce hydrologically comparable reference shortlists. Trust: moderate; it rests on 12 convenience windows (two with fewer than 20 retained queries) and an inventory-label endpoint.
- Practitioners: the paper correctly states that neither embedding distance nor NWI agreement establishes reference condition; practical judgment (keep explicit hydrology and field verification) would not change, because that is already standard practice.
- Exploratory positive cases (C09, C12, C13; whitened distances, 2021 embeddings, other-window TESSERA) should not change judgment: they were chosen after earlier outcomes, have intervals crossing zero, and their differences lie within the seed/null-perturbation range.

## Accuracy of the abstract
- The numbers in the abstract match the archived outputs and the re-run.
- The abstract does not state an answer to the research question. It reports the prespecified point estimates and then lists about ten exploratory numbers, so a reader cannot tell whether the conclusion is "embeddings help" or "an increment is not established". The plan (plan-v6) said main incremental claims should be qualified if comparable to null-feature perturbations; the abstract does not make that qualification explicitly, and omits the planned permutation control and the pooled estimates.
- Overgeneralization: the paper's scope statements are generally careful (no national, field-hydrology or user-benefit claims). The phrase "further limiting generalization across embedding years" for a 2021 result that is better than 2024 is unclear, and year differences are within seed-level variation.

Limitation: this file reflects one reviewer's judgment after a 3-hour review; it is not evidence of how actual readers would respond.

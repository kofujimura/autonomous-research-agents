Title: "Training Point Allocation and Recovery of Small Wetland Patches from Satellite Embeddings"

Question (quoted): "How does training-point allocation interact with annual satellite embeddings versus seasonal optical predictors in recovering small mapped wetland patches at a common reference-upland false-positive area rate, and can image-derived allocation recover an oracle object-frame benefit?"

## My expected answer and confidence

1. Allocation main effect. Patch-first (object-balanced) allocation of training positions, compared with pixel-uniform sampling from wetland pixels, upweights small patches in training and should modestly raise the recovery of small mapped patches at a fixed false-positive rate. I expect a small positive effect (on the order of a few percentage points of small-patch recall), with a cost for large patches / pixel-level recall possibly small. Confidence ~0.55 that the sign is positive and statistically distinguishable in a reasonable sample; ~0.35 that the effect is practically large (>5 pp).
2. Interaction with predictors. AEF annual embeddings (10 m, built from multi-sensor time series with spatial context) are likely to be somewhat smoother than per-pixel seasonal Sentinel-2 composites; I expect small-patch recovery with embeddings to be similar or somewhat lower than optical predictors at matched FPR for the smallest patches, and similar or higher in aggregate. Whether the allocation effect differs between predictors: I expect any interaction to be small and hard to detect (confidence ~0.6 that the interaction is small/unclear rather than a clear reversal).
3. Image-derived allocation. An allocation frame derived from the imagery itself (e.g., candidate objects from segmentation or a first-pass classifier) will likely recover only part, or none, of an oracle (reference-inventory-based) object-frame benefit, because the small wetlands that are hard to see in the imagery are exactly those the image-derived frame misses. Confidence ~0.6 that it recovers less than half of the oracle benefit.
4. Overall, I expect results to depend strongly on region (prairie potholes vs forested/northeastern wetlands), the definition of "small", the reference inventory's date/minimum mapping unit, and the chosen FPR operating point; I expect geographic heterogeneity to be large relative to the allocation effect.

## How researchers and practitioners would judge this now (concretely)

- Wetland-mapping researchers and agency practitioners (e.g., state DNR wetland-inventory updates such as Minnesota's NWI update, USFWS NWI, Wisconsin Wetland Inventory) already regard small/isolated wetlands as systematically under-mapped and treat 10 m optical data as limited for patches below roughly 0.1–0.5 ha; they increasingly rely on lidar-derived terrain metrics, high-resolution aerial imagery and object-based image analysis (OBIA) rather than 10 m pixels.
- Remote-sensing researchers know that training-sample allocation (proportional vs. equal vs. object-balanced; spatial clustering; class imbalance) changes classifier outputs; studies such as Colditz (2015) and Millard & Richardson (2015) on training-sample allocation/selection for random forests, and the good-practice accuracy-assessment literature (Olofsson et al. 2014; Stehman), are the standard references. Practitioners would expect "sample small objects more" to help small-object recall at some cost elsewhere, and would not consider that surprising by itself.
- For satellite embeddings (AlphaEarth Foundations / Google Satellite Embedding dataset, released 2025), the current practitioner judgment is enthusiasm about convenience and aggregate accuracy, with open concerns about spatial smoothing, small features, and representativeness of sparse-point evaluations. A study quantifying small-patch recovery at matched false-positive rates would be useful to them if it is region-diverse and reports the effect size relative to regional variation.
- Readers would likely judge an "oracle" allocation that uses the reference inventory as not deployable where the inventory is missing, so the practically relevant part is item 3 (image-derived allocation), and they would expect that part to be weak.

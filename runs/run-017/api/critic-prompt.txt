You are a critic of research topics. Below are topic candidates for a study that an AI agent will carry out autonomously. Point out the weaknesses of each candidate.

The main readers of this study are researchers working on the social problem addressed and practitioners in government, business, communities and other settings (both humans and AIs). A good topic is one where readers' understanding or judgment changes between before and after reading the results. Each study takes 12 hours and is carried out within the range of local compute, public data and a small amount of AI API use.

For each candidate, look for weaknesses from the following points of view.
1. Effect on readers: Which understanding or judgment, concretely, would researchers and practitioners who read the results change? If they would not change any, say so.
2. Generality: Do the results hold beyond a particular tool, library version or dataset? Will they not become outdated in a few months?
3. Realism: Is there a risk that the effect appears only under artificial conditions or with inputs that are rare in reality? Are realistic inputs excluded from the targets?
4. Novelty: Is there close prior work or a known result (including what is already known from official statistics, reports or white papers)? Does it amount to no more than re-confirming a known concern with numbers?
5. Unpredictability: Could the result be largely predicted without experiments?
6. Size of the question: Has it become a solid but small question?

Before naming close prior work, confirm it by web search. Also look for literature and preprints from 2025 onward. Include the standard methods and classic studies of the field. Also check whether the same problem has been studied under a different name in other fields.

For each candidate, write in the following format.
- The most serious weakness (with the number of the point of view above)
- Prior work that seems close (authors, title, year, URL; write "unconfirmed" for any you could not confirm by web search)
- A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds
- A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality (at most one, within the scope of that candidate; do not propose new topics)

Do not rank the candidates. You have not been told which candidate is planned to be adopted.

Candidate list:
C01. Question: For multi-day heat-protection planning, does preserving ensemble temporal dependence improve sustained-hot-night forecasts after matching marginal calibration?
Method outline: Verify run probabilities and cost-loss activation outcomes against observed/reanalysis temperatures; compare intact learned weather trajectories, shuffled marginals, ensemble copula coupling and observational analog reconstruction under equal marginal information.
Closest work and difference: Price et al., Probabilistic weather forecasting with machine learning (Nature, 2024, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9) already evaluates extremes and joint/spatial outcomes; the candidate focuses on temporal heat-spell decisions at matched marginal skill. Schefzik et al., Uncertainty Quantification in Complex Simulation Models Using Ensemble Copula Coupling (2013, doi:10.1214/13-STS443) already establishes dependence preservation; the candidate claims no new principle. Wessel et al., Probabilistic AI models outperform numerical weather prediction models on record breaking heat extremes (arXiv:2610.20519, 2026), is very recent close work; distinction from its broader extreme-event verification needs scrutiny.

C02. Question: Do learned weather ensemble forecasts selected for pointwise wind skill support adequate buffers during prolonged regional wind droughts?
Method outline: Use public forecasts and reference weather with transparent wind-to-power sensitivity analysis; compare cumulative-deficit reserve scheduling with climatology, analogs and dependence-aware calibration, rather than just instantaneous power scores.
Closest work and difference: Price et al. (2024, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9) already evaluates regionally summed wind power. Bracken et al., Standardized benchmark of historical compound wind and solar energy droughts across the Continental United States (2024, doi:10.1016/j.renene.2023.119550) and Seasonal compound renewable energy droughts in the United States (2025, doi:10.1088/2753-3751/adc8ad) already characterize drought duration and load coincidence. Candidate difference is forecast-conditioned cumulative-deficit decisions, not another drought climatology.

C03. Question: Do smooth maps produced from annual satellite embeddings preserve small isolated wetlands under spatially held-out evaluation, and can equal-budget object-aware sampling improve retention relative to sampling homogeneous interior pixels?
Method outline: Use public annual AlphaEarth embeddings and independently aerial/lidar-interpreted wetland polygons, matched seasonal optical predictors and conventional smoothing controls. Cross representation with homogeneous-interior, area-balanced and object-balanced sample allocation; hold out geographic blocks; evaluate wetland-area accuracy alongside patch detection/coverage by size and isolation. Quantify rasterization floors, boundary tolerance and reference-date uncertainty. Treat map smoothness as a diagnostic, not ground truth, and avoid attributing associations causally to latent context.
Closest work and difference: Ryan et al., Streamlining Wetland Vegetation Mapping with AlphaEarth Embeddings: Comparable Accuracy to Traditional Methods with Cleaner Maps and Minimal Preprocessing (2026, doi:10.3390/rs18020293) selects homogeneous cluster/inlier samples and interprets smoother maps/larger patches as useful. Brown et al., AlphaEarth Foundations (2025, arXiv:2507.22291v2) establishes broad sparse-pixel mapping performance with strong baselines. Halabisky et al., Object-based classification of semi-arid wetlands (2011, Journal of Applied Remote Sensing 5, 053511) already reports errors concentrated in small wetlands. Candidate difference is the joint sampling-design/representation/object-fidelity comparison under equal label budgets, not discovery that small wetlands are hard to map or that pixel accuracy can hide rare objects.

C06. Question: For multi-day electricity-demand planning, does selecting a zero-shot forecaster by short-interval probabilistic accuracy also select the most accurate aggregate-demand distribution?
Method outline: On public aggregate load, compare direct aggregate forecasts with aggregation of coherent, independent and empirical-copula scenarios from small public foundation models, using seasonal/ETS and probabilistic reconciliation baselines. Evaluate both proper scores at matched horizons and transparent cumulative-demand planning loss.
Closest work and difference: Ansari et al., Chronos: Learning the Language of Time Series (2024, arXiv:2403.07815) evaluates zero-shot per-series forecasting. Jeon et al., Reconciliation of probabilistic forecasts with an application to wind power (2018 preprint arXiv:1808.02635; related published EJOR 2019 work) and Nystrup et al., Temporal hierarchies with autocorrelation for load forecasting (2020, doi:10.1016/j.ejor.2019.07.061) already establish temporal aggregation/dependence methods. Candidate difference may be limited to the zero-shot model regime; it would not claim a new reconciliation principle.

C08. Question: In public environmental measurement tables, does structure-preserving optical extraction improve interval-based screening decisions beyond what text-level accuracy predicts?
Method outline: Pair publicly reusable PDF tables with verified structured source measurements; compare a small public unified OCR model, conventional OCR/table extraction and conservative interval propagation. Score inequality signs, detection-limit relations and footnotes against source data; distinguish extraction from any legal compliance judgement.
Closest work and difference: Wei et al., General OCR Theory: Towards OCR-2.0 via a Unified End-to-end Model (2024, arXiv:2409.01704), Smock et al., PubTables-1M (CVPR 2022), and Ouyang et al., OmniDocBench: Benchmarking Diverse PDF Document Parsing with Comprehensive Annotations (CVPR 2025) already evaluate text, formulas and table structures. Environmental nondetect handling is established. Candidate difference is decision-sensitive interval validation in real source-paired reports rather than a generic character/structure benchmark.

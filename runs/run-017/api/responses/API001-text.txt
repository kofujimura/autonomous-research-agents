I assume the 12 hours include obtaining, aligning and validating the data—not just running models. The references below were confirmed by web search, including 2025–2026 work; linked titles provide the requested URLs. These are critiques of the proposed questions, not predictions that their methods will fail.

## C01 — Temporal dependence in sustained-hot-night forecasts

- **The most serious weakness — (1) Effect on readers, linked to (4) novelty and (5) predictability.**  
  The useful question is not simply whether temporal dependence matters, but **whether retaining a learned model’s dependence changes protection decisions compared with inexpensive, established reconstruction methods**. Beating shuffled marginals would not establish that. Conversely, preserving an inaccurate learned dependence structure need not improve forecasts. ECC and the Schaake shuffle already make the separation between marginal calibration and dependence explicit. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149?utm_source=openai))

  The comparison also needs sharper specification. Equal calibration scores do not mean identical marginal distributions. Moreover, **if ECC uses the same learned ranks and the same marginal samples as the calibrated intact trajectories, those two arms can coincide by construction**—a methodological inference from ECC, not an empirical discovery. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149?utm_source=openai))

  **Realism (3):** if the study uses the original GenCast’s 12-hour output, sampled temperatures cannot simply be treated as local nightly minima. That could change the event being forecast while retaining the “hot-night” label. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9?utm_source=openai)) **Generality and size (2, 6):** one archive, one threshold and a few spells would establish only a local dependence-template comparison. Researchers would change little beyond their assessment of that particular model; practitioners might change nothing unless activation timing, duration or resource commitments actually differ.

- **Prior work that seems close**
  - **Schefzik, Thorarinsdottir and Gneiting (2013), [*Uncertainty Quantification in Complex Simulation Models Using Ensemble Copula Coupling*](https://doi.org/10.1214/13-STS443).** The central marginal-calibration/dependence-preservation framework is already established here. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149?utm_source=openai))
  - **Clark et al. (2004), [*The Schaake Shuffle: A Method for Reconstructing Space–Time Variability in Forecasted Precipitation and Temperature Fields*](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70027101).** Classic hydrometeorological treatment of the same problem under “space–time reconstruction,” including temporal persistence. ([pubs.usgs.gov](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70027101?utm_source=openai))
  - **Price et al. (2024 online; 2025 journal issue), [*Probabilistic weather forecasting with machine learning*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9).** Published online December 4, 2024; already evaluates extremes and joint outcomes, although not the proposed matched-marginal hot-night decision experiment. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9?utm_source=openai))
  - **Wessel, Spuler, Ferro, Kretschmer and Sippel (2026), [*Probabilistic AI models outperform numerical weather prediction models on record breaking heat extremes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20519).** Confirmed preprint, submitted October 14, 2026. It already includes hour-matched nighttime extremes, tail calibration, postprocessed NWP and station verification. The remaining distinction is sustained-run probabilities and decisions—not nighttime extremes alone. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20519))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**  
  Intact trajectories substantially beat shuffled forecasts, but observational reconstruction performs equally well, and all credible methods activate protection on the same dates. The study confirms a known statistical requirement without changing either forecast procurement or protection planning.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**  
  Make this **one deployment-style activation replay**: use an existing station-matched archive with a defensible local-night target, identical marginal samples, and one specified multi-night commitment rule. Evaluate whether the dependence template changes commitments over complete seasons, including non-events—not merely whether it improves run-probability scores.

## C02 — Wind forecasts and buffers for prolonged regional droughts

- **The most serious weakness — (3) Realism: a wind-energy deficit is not yet an adequacy deficit.**  
  A cumulative shortfall below a wind-production threshold does not itself determine the required buffer. The proposed experiment could be internally correct while answering a different question from “will this system have enough energy?” Bracken’s work already distinguishes renewable droughts from droughts compounded by high load; Price’s wind-power experiment is explicitly a simplified weather-to-power calculation. ([nysrc.org](https://www.nysrc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/1-s2.0-S0960148123014659-main.pdf?utm_source=openai))

  The consequential reader change **(1)** would be: “For this operating situation, select forecast A rather than B, or procure this different buffer.” That requires explicit assumptions about demand, other available supply, buffer power and energy limits, initial charge, and opportunities to update the decision. Wind-to-power sensitivity analysis alone does not resolve those assumptions.

  **Novelty and predictability (4, 5):** marginal forecast verification overlooking temporal scenario quality is already an established concern in wind-energy forecasting and stochastic programming. ([orbit.dtu.dk](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/evaluating-the-quality-of-scenarios-of-short-term-wind-power-gene/?utm_source=openai)) **Generality and size (2, 6):** if the result depends on one turbine curve, fleet and arbitrary drought threshold, it becomes a small sensitivity exercise. Within 12 hours, another danger is treating many overlapping forecast windows from a few droughts as many independent demonstrations.

- **Prior work that seems close**
  - **Pinson et al. (2009), [*From Probabilistic Forecasts to Statistical Scenarios of Short-term Wind Power Production*](https://doi.org/10.1002/we.284).** A standard scenario-construction reference connecting per-horizon probabilistic forecasts to temporal trajectories. ([pierrepinson.com](https://pierrepinson.com/publications?utm_source=openai))
  - **Pinson and Girard (2012), [*Evaluating the quality of scenarios of short-term wind power generation*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2011.11.004).** Particularly close: criticizes marginal-only evaluation and develops multivariate and event-based verification for scenarios used in multistage decisions. This is the same concern expressed through operations research and stochastic programming. ([orbit.dtu.dk](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/evaluating-the-quality-of-scenarios-of-short-term-wind-power-gene/?utm_source=openai))
  - **Price et al. (2024 online; 2025 issue), [*Probabilistic weather forecasting with machine learning*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9).** Already evaluates regional wind-power distributions; temporal cumulative deficits remain a distinct target. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9?utm_source=openai))
  - **Bracken et al. (2024), [*Standardized benchmark of historical compound wind and solar energy droughts across the Continental United States*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.renene.2023.119550).** Already examines duration, intensity, regional variation and coincident load, explicitly motivated by storage and grid planning. ([nysrc.org](https://www.nysrc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/1-s2.0-S0960148123014659-main.pdf?utm_source=openai))
  - **Bracken et al. (2025), [*Seasonal compound renewable energy droughts in the United States*](https://doi.org/10.1088/2753-3751/adc8ad).** Extends the drought literature to seasonal compound conditions; it is background on the hazard, not a substitute for forecast-conditioned decision evaluation. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1088/2753-3751%2Fadc8ad?utm_source=openai))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**  
  Dependence-aware forecasts produce better cumulative wind-deficit distributions, but available dispatchable supply covers every tested episode. Alternatively, the apparent buffer benefit disappears under another plausible power-conversion assumption. Neither result establishes a change in operational adequacy.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**  
  Turn the cumulative-deficit calculation into **one transparent, rolling buffer-operation replay** for a public regional dataset: explicitly fix non-wind supply assumptions and buffer constraints, update forecasts as they would become available, and measure realized shortages and unused buffer. This preserves the candidate’s scope without requiring a full power-system simulator.

## C03 — Small wetlands, annual embeddings and object-aware sampling

- **The most serious weakness — (3) Realism: “equal label budget” may conceal unequal information and acquisition cost.**  
  Object-balanced sampling needs a way to identify distinct wetland objects before selecting labels. If the experiment obtains that information from complete reference polygons, it may give the object-aware arm an expensive sampling frame for free. Equal numbers of sampled pixels would then not imply equally feasible mapping workflows. Reference-data cost, sampling units and uncertainty are longstanding components of rigorous map assessment. ([nottingham-repository.worktribe.com](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/728216/good-practices-for-estimating-area-and-assessing-accuracy-of-land-change?utm_source=openai))

  Geographic holdouts, rasterization floors and boundary tolerances are useful safeguards, but they do not address whether the inventory itself misses difficult objects. Halabisky et al. found small omitted wetlands that were not identifiable in their aerial imagery, illustrating why “independently interpreted” does not automatically mean complete. ([cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com](https://cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.uw.edu/dist/a/122/files/2017/12/Halabisky-et-al_2011_Object-based-classification-of-semi-arid-wetlands-zirr5s.pdf))

  **Reader effect (1):** the relevant change is whether a mapping agency should allocate scarce interpretation effort differently—not merely whether balanced training improves recall. **Novelty and predictability (4, 5):** small-object omission and misleading aggregate segmentation scores are established; the genuinely uncertain contribution is the *representation-by-sampling interaction*. ([cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com](https://cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.uw.edu/dist/a/122/files/2017/12/Halabisky-et-al_2011_Object-based-classification-of-semi-arid-wetlands-zirr5s.pdf)) **Generality and size (2, 6):** a result limited to one wetland type, embedding release and polygon inventory remains a local benchmark. “Cleaner maps are not always better” alone would be too small a conclusion.

- **Prior work that seems close**
  - **Ryan, Powell, Ling and Wen (2026), [*Streamlining Wetland Vegetation Mapping with AlphaEarth Embeddings: Comparable Accuracy to Traditional Methods with Cleaner Maps and Minimal Preprocessing*](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18020293).** Direct motivation: homogeneous sample selection and favorable interpretation of smoother maps. It does not establish retention of isolated wetland objects. ([mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/18/2?page=2&utm_source=openai))
  - **Brown et al. (2025), [*AlphaEarth Foundations: An embedding field model for accurate and efficient global mapping from sparse label data*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22291v2).** Establishes broad sparse-label mapping performance; representation efficiency by itself is therefore not the new contribution. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22291v2))
  - **Halabisky, Moskal and Hall (2011), [*Object-based classification of semi-arid wetlands*](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.3563569).** Classic object-based wetland mapping study documenting small-wetland omissions and distinguishing detection from delineation. ([cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com](https://cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.uw.edu/dist/a/122/files/2017/12/Halabisky-et-al_2011_Object-based-classification-of-semi-arid-wetlands-zirr5s.pdf))
  - **Yuan et al. (2026), [*Wetland mapping from sparse annotations with satellite image time series and temporal-aware segment anything model*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11400).** Recent neighboring work on sparse supervision, temporal information and fragmented wetland masks—not an exact duplicate of the proposed factorial comparison. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11400?utm_source=openai))
  - **Olofsson et al. (2014), [*Good practices for estimating area and assessing accuracy of land change*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2014.02.015).** Standard sampling, reference-data and area-estimation framework. ([nottingham-repository.worktribe.com](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/728216/good-practices-for-estimating-area-and-assessing-accuracy-of-land-change?utm_source=openai))
  - **Maier-Hein et al. (2024), [*Metrics reloaded: recommendations for image analysis validation*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-02151-z).** Cross-field overlap from biomedical image analysis: small structures, object detection, boundary uncertainty and unsuitable aggregate segmentation metrics. ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11182665/))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**  
  Object balancing raises small-patch recall for embeddings and conventional predictors equally, but requires an essentially complete wetland inventory and creates substantially more false-positive patches. The result neither identifies an embedding-specific limitation nor establishes a cost-effective mapping improvement.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**  
  Make the existing factorial experiment a **deployment-realistic label-budget comparison**: charge explicitly for obtaining the object sampling frame, restrict sampling information to the training geography, and compare retained small wetlands at a fixed false-positive inspection burden. Existing verified polygons are essential; creating that reference resource autonomously should not be hidden inside the 12-hour experiment.

## C06 — Short-interval accuracy versus aggregate-demand distributions

- **The most serious weakness — (4) Novelty: the zero-shot setting no longer provides much separation by itself.**  
  The mathematical issue is already clear: marginal forecast quality does not determine the distribution of a sum, because the covariance terms remain unspecified. More importantly, recent work explicitly makes this argument for time-series foundation models and operational tasks. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19345))

  **Unpredictability (5):** the existence of possible ranking reversals needs no experiment; their frequency, magnitude and operational consequences do. **Reader effect (1):** the study would matter if it changes a selection rule—such as whether to select a backbone by hourly score, validate daily totals directly, or add a cheap dependence correction. A table showing different winners at different aggregations is not sufficient.

  **Realism (3):** direct and aggregated forecasts must have the same information cutoff and comparable calendar-history access; otherwise different context lengths can masquerade as an aggregation effect. Also, algebraic coherence—samples summing correctly—is not evidence that their dependence is realistic. The existing reconciliation literature already distinguishes aggregation constraints from forecast accuracy. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.02635?utm_source=openai)) **Generality and size (2, 6):** a ranking of a few small model releases on one load series is short-lived. A load-history-only experiment also needs to limit its claim to that information regime rather than electricity planning generally.

- **Prior work that seems close**
  - **Ansari et al. (2024), [*Chronos: Learning the Language of Time Series*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815).** Establishes the zero-shot probabilistic forecasting setting, not the proposed aggregate-selection result. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815?utm_source=openai))
  - **Jeon, Panagiotelis and Petropoulos (2018 preprint), [*Reconciliation of probabilistic forecasts with an application to wind power*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.02635).** Direct precedent for probabilistic temporal reconciliation and improvements across aggregation levels. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.02635?utm_source=openai))
  - **Nystrup, Lindström, Pinson and Madsen (2020), [*Temporal hierarchies with autocorrelation for load forecasting*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2019.07.061).** Particularly close application and mechanism: load forecasting with temporal aggregation and auto-/cross-covariance-aware reconciliation. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1016/J.EJOR.2019.07.061?utm_source=openai))
  - **Perez-Diaz, Loach, Toutoungi and Middleton (2025), [*Foundation Model Forecasts: Form and Function*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19345).** The strongest additional novelty challenge: directly discusses foundation-model output types, temporal dependence, aggregate risk and task-aligned evaluation. It is not, however, the proposed electricity-load experiment. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19345))
  - **Simeone (2026), [*Time Series Foundation Models for Energy Load Forecasting on Consumer Hardware: A Multi-Dimensional Zero-Shot Benchmark*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10848).** Already combines low-compute zero-shot load forecasting, calibration, distribution shifts and decision support; aggregate-distribution selection must be the clear distinction. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10848))
  - **Rostami-Tabar, Babai, Ali and Boylan (2019), [*The impact of temporal aggregation on supply chains with ARMA(1,1) demand processes*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2018.09.010).** The analogous problem appears in inventory research as lead-time demand forecasting and the relationship between forecast accuracy and operational performance. ([sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221718307562?utm_source=openai))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**  
  Rankings reverse statistically, but planning-loss differences are negligible, or a simple empirical-copula correction removes them for every model. The main conclusion becomes “evaluate the quantity you actually need,” without a consequential new selection rule.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**  
  Evaluate **selection regret rather than a leaderboard**: using chronological validation, select the model by short-interval scores, then measure the additional held-out cumulative-demand planning loss relative to aggregate-targeted selection, including whether inexpensive reconciliation removes that regret. This tests a transferable workflow decision while retaining the original question.

## C08 — Environmental-table extraction and interval-based screening

- **The most serious weakness — (1) Effect on readers: improved semantic fidelity is not yet evidence for a different automation policy.**  
  The consequential judgment is whether practitioners can safely automate more screening, or reduce review effort at a fixed error tolerance. Demonstrating that lost inequality signs matter more than their contribution to character-error rate is unlikely to change expert understanding: preserving censoring information and validating extracted analytical results are already established concerns. ([agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1029/WR024i012p01997?utm_source=openai))

  Conservative interval propagation also creates a potential trivial success: fewer wrong decisions because almost everything becomes “indeterminate.” That must be distinguished from genuinely better extraction through an error-versus-coverage or review-burden comparison—the established selective-prediction perspective. ([papers.nips.cc](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html?utm_source=openai))

  **Realism (3):** source-paired reports may overrepresent clean, digitally generated documents. Unless native-PDF extraction is included where available, rasterizing them could manufacture an OCR problem. The structured source must also match the report’s sample identities, revisions, units and qualifiers; it is not automatically a perfect reference merely because it is structured.

  **Novelty, predictability and durability (4–6):** “semantic errors outweigh character accuracy” is predictable; a one-model, one-template demonstration would be small and release-sensitive. The durable contribution would be a decision-error taxonomy and a validated review rule. Without an already identified paired corpus, reference verification could consume the entire 12 hours.

- **Prior work that seems close**
  - **Wei et al. (2024), [*General OCR Theory: Towards OCR-2.0 via a Unified End-to-end Model*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01704).** Establishes the unified extraction model, not environmental decision validity. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01704?utm_source=openai))
  - **Smock, Pesala and Abraham (2022), [*PubTables-1M: Towards Comprehensive Table Extraction From Unstructured Documents*](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Smock_PubTables-1M_Towards_Comprehensive_Table_Extraction_From_Unstructured_Documents_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf).** Standard table-detection, structure and functional-analysis baseline; the candidate must demonstrate value beyond these structural evaluations. ([openaccess.thecvf.com](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Smock_PubTables-1M_Towards_Comprehensive_Table_Extraction_From_Unstructured_Documents_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf?utm_source=openai))
  - **Ouyang et al. (2025), [*OmniDocBench: Benchmarking Diverse PDF Document Parsing with Comprehensive Annotations*](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Ouyang_OmniDocBench_Benchmarking_Diverse_PDF_Document_Parsing_with_Comprehensive_Annotations_CVPR_2025_paper.html).** Already evaluates varied document types with task- and attribute-level analysis. Environmental screening consequences remain distinct. ([openaccess.thecvf.com](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Ouyang_OmniDocBench_Benchmarking_Diverse_PDF_Document_Parsing_with_Comprehensive_Annotations_CVPR_2025_paper.html?utm_source=openai))
  - **Helsel and Cohn (1988), [*Estimation of descriptive statistics for multiply censored water quality data*](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR024i012p01997).** Classic environmental-statistics precedent: less-than observations and multiple reporting limits cannot safely be reduced to ordinary numeric values. ([agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1029/WR024i012p01997?utm_source=openai))
  - **Geifman and El-Yaniv (2017), [*Selective Classification for Deep Neural Networks*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500).** Cross-field precedent under “reject option” or “selective prediction”: reducing errors must be evaluated alongside the fraction of cases still handled automatically. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500?utm_source=openai))
  - **EarthScience Information Systems / ESdat (2026), [*AI for Environmental Data Extraction from PDF Reports*](https://www.mygeoworld.com/resources/blog/2055-ai-for-environmental-data-extraction-from-pdf-reports).** A practitioner/vendor article, not independent validation. Nevertheless, it already describes analytical-result extraction combined with coded checks, traceability and expert validation; those workflow concerns are not new. ([mygeoworld.com](https://www.mygeoworld.com/resources/blog/2055-ai-for-environmental-data-extraction-from-pdf-reports?utm_source=openai))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**  
  The structure-aware pipeline makes fewer screening mistakes, but only by sending substantially more tables to review. Alternatively, the benefit appears mainly on PDFs whose structured data or reliable native text were already available, leaving the difficult legacy-report use case untested.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**  
  Make this **a selective-screening evaluation at fixed review burden** on a small, already verified, multi-template corpus. Apply the same interval logic to every extractor, retain native-text extraction where applicable, and measure incorrect clearance, incorrect escalation and abstention separately. That isolates extraction value without expanding into legal compliance judgments.

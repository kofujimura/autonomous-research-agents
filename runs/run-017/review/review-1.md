## 1. Summary of the paper
The paper asks whether the way 2,000 training positions are allocated (pixel-uniform within class, "area", versus patch-first "oracle object" allocation using the inventory's polygons, plus image-derived proposal/uncertainty rules) changes the recovery of small (0.2–1 ha) National Wetlands Inventory palustrine patches differently for annual AlphaEarth embeddings (AEF v2.1, 2020) and seasonal Sentinel-2 predictors, at a matched 5% reference-upland false-positive area rate. In the pre-specified original split (four NWI projects, 16 test cores, 227 small patches, RF, five seeds), oracle allocation raised embedding recovery from 44.8% to 57.6% and optical recovery from 36.4% to 37.6% (interaction 11.6 pp, conditional 95% interval [4.9, 18.5]); the optical-derived proposal rule did not reproduce the embedding gain. Planned and exploratory checks show that validation-selected thresholds overshoot the target FPR, that the interaction is small/uncertain under whole-region transfer, and that pooling the original split with three additional fixed split rotations attenuates the interaction to 4.5 pp [0.3, 8.3]. Exploratory loss-weighting and embedding-guided uncertainty sampling recover part of the oracle gain. The paper limits its target to agreement with an existing inventory, not ecological completeness or annotation cost.

## 2. Overall verdict
Major revision. A re-run by another agent on the same machine reproduced every reported number, interval and table from the saved predictions (all ten claims match). The study is carefully pre-registered, the timeline shows plans preceding results, and most limitations are disclosed. However, the abstract and discussion lead with one of the largest available estimates of the interaction (11.6 pp: original split, 5% pooled FPR, ≥50% coverage; it is the largest of the four splits) and frame the optical predictors as not benefiting, while the saved results show that (a) across all four fixed splits the oracle allocation benefits both predictors (embeddings +8.4 pp, optical +3.9 pp) and the embedding-specific excess is about 4.5 pp, (b) other pre-registered operating points and coverage definitions give substantially smaller interactions, and (c) several pre-registered analyses are computed but not reported. These are fixable within 2 hours by re-weighting the text and adding one table from existing outputs.

## 5. Findings

### R1-1
- Severity: Moderate
- Type: Overclaim
- Target: paper.md Abstract, §4.1 (Tables 2–3), §5 Discussion (first paragraph); claims.jsonl C01 (reader_implication), C06
- Content:
  - Observed facts. (1) Original split, pooled 5% test-standardized FPR, ≥50% coverage: embeddings 44.8→57.6% (+12.8 pp [8.4, 17.2]); optical 36.4→37.6% (+1.2 pp [−4.5, 6.1]); interaction 11.6 pp [4.9, 18.5] (analysis/summary.json primary_contrasts). (2) The three new fixed rotations give interactions 4.7 [−8.1, 11.3], −0.4 [−5.0, 4.8] and 1.2 [−6.4, 9.3] pp (paper §4.5 table); two of the three point estimates lie below the lower bound (4.9) of the original-split interval. (3) In those rotations the optical oracle gain is positive in every case: 4.5 [0.6, 8.2], 2.2 [−0.1, 4.2], 6.7 [1.2, 15.9] pp (analysis/summary.json secondary_condition_contrasts, scopes rotation1–3). (4) Pooled over all four splits (978 small patches) the component gains are embeddings +8.4 pp [4.5, 12.1] and optical +3.9 pp [1.6, 6.2], interaction 4.5 [0.3, 8.3] (analysis/robustness/summary.json, rotations_all; Appendix D shows the arm means 41.7→50.0 and 37.9→41.8). (5) My own pooling of only the three new rotations, using the same count-summing and equal-region weighting (which reproduces the paper's 4.5 pp when the original split is included), gives embeddings +7.0, optical +4.6, interaction 2.4 pp (review-017/verification/runs/reviewer-checks/definition_sensitivity_*.txt; point estimates only, no interval). (6) The bootstrap (code/summarize.py primary_bootstrap) resamples, within each region, 4 test cores with replacement and the 5 seeds; training cores and thresholds are fixed. (7) In the same original split, under the pre-registered validation-threshold replay the interaction is 4.5 pp [−2.4, 12.0] (see R1-2).
  - Inferences. The finding that is stable across splits is that patch-first allocation raises within-region small-patch recovery for both predictors, roughly twice as much for embeddings; the original split gives the largest interaction of the four splits and the smallest optical gain. With 4 clusters per region the percentile bootstrap cannot represent variation due to which cores are in training/test, which the rotations show is large relative to the interval width; the original-split interval should therefore not be read as the uncertainty of the effect. The abstract sentence "compared with 36.4% to 37.6% for seasonal optical predictors" and Discussion's "The observed embedding improvement under object allocation is larger than the corresponding optical improvement" are true for the original split, but readers will take them as "optical predictors do not benefit", which the other splits contradict.
- Requested action: (Priority 1) In the abstract and §5, report the pooled four-split component gains (embeddings +8.4 pp, optical +3.9 pp, interaction 4.5 pp) at least as prominently as the original-split numbers, and say explicitly that optical predictors also gained in every new rotation. (Priority 1) State in §3.4/§4.5 that the conditional interval resamples only 4 test cores per region and that two of three new-split estimates fall below the original interval, so the interval understates split-to-split variability. (Priority 2) Revise C01's reader_implication and the Discussion so that the practical message is "patch-first allocation improved small-patch recovery for both predictors within these projects; the embedding-specific excess was a few points and split-dependent". No new fits are needed.
- Feasibility of the action: can be done within the research environment (text changes from existing outputs).

## 6. Value to readers
(Full text: review-017/reader_value.md; this is my self-report, not a measurement of readers' behaviour.)
- Compared with my prior expectation (saved before reading the paper), the direction of the allocation effect was as expected, the original-split interaction (11.6 pp) was larger than expected, and the totality of splits (4.5 pp pooled; 2.4 pp in the three new rotations alone) was close to my expectation of a small, split-dependent interaction. The partial recovery by embedding-guided uncertainty sampling (C08) was better than I expected.
- Judgments that could change for wetland-inventory practitioners and remote-sensing researchers: (1) pooled pixel accuracy/IoU can stay flat while small-patch recovery changes by more than 10 pp (C10) — high trust as a descriptive observation; (2) polygon-first allocation of training positives can raise within-project small-patch recovery at a fixed FPR (C01, C06, C09) — moderate trust for the within-project direction, low trust for the size of the embedding-specific advantage; not for new regions (C04); (3) validation thresholds do not hold their FPR on test cores and transfer between projects is poor (C03, Appendix D) — high trust as an observation for these data; (4) the optical-teacher proposal rule did not help, embedding-guided uncertainty partly did (C02, C08) — moderate/exploratory.
- The abstract's individual statements match the outputs for the conditions they name, but it over-weights the original split (optical presented as not benefiting) and understates the transfer result (R1-1, R1-3). It does not over-generalise geographically. A practitioner reading only the abstract would overestimate how specific the allocation benefit is to embeddings.

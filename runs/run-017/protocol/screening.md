# Six-point screening of divergent candidates

All entries below are decisions made during selection; discarded candidates remain recorded. A technology is not necessary merely because it can produce a proxy. The closest-prior-work checks are continuing for the five finalists C01, C02, C03, C06 and C08. Feasibility remains provisional before the pilots.

## C01
1. Same study without capability? Coherent traditional ensembles already permit the generic study; the new question must concern whether cheap learned scenarios preserve decision-relevant persistence. Otherwise decoration.
2. Existing method/problem? ECC and Schaake shuffle already preserve dependence; GenCast already evaluates joint outcomes. A matched-marginal hot-night run-length comparison could differ but the gap may be small.
3. Analytically predetermined? Shuffling changes dependence by definition; the sign and size of skill/value changes against observed spells is empirical. Do not report the algebra as a finding.
4. AI ground truth? Reanalysis / station observations permit forecast verification; operational heat-health outcomes are not measured.
5. Executable? Only published trajectories; pilot will check. No new cloud billing permitted.
6. Reader change? If benefit survives strong reconstruction baselines, retain trajectories in heat warning feeds; if not, less expensive marginal feeds may suffice within tested conditions.
Constraint origin: no. Retain for finalist check.

## C02
1. Same study without capability? Traditional ensembles can study wind droughts. The question needs to distinguish adequacy of learned scenarios, not just restate dependence matters.
2. Existing method/problem? GenCast already evaluates spatially aggregated wind power; energy drought forecasting and storage sizing are established. Only cumulative-deficit decisions may remain distinct.
3. Analytically predetermined? Cumulative risk depends on correlation, but comparative observed scheduling performance cannot be derived from that fact alone.
4. AI ground truth? Reanalysis wind and observed aggregate wind generation can check the proxy; converting wind to power needs a sensitivity check.
5. Executable? Published forecast availability is uncertain, shared pilot P02.
6. Reader change? Success would justify scenario-based reserve decisions; failure would restrict claims inferred from usual forecast scores.
Constraint origin: no. Retain finalist, with serious closeness concern.

## C03
1. Same study without capability? Small-wetland remote sensing is old. The candidate is revised to ask whether the recently promoted smooth embedding-derived maps preserve small isolated objects; this conditional map-property question requires the representation outputs.
2. Existing method/problem? Ryan et al. 2026 explicitly report smoother maps and larger patches; Brown et al. 2025 report broad mapping skill. Object-level fidelity of small isolated wetlands against matched spectral and smoothing controls was not found in the initial search. Do not assert absence.
3. Analytically predetermined? Area accuracy can hide small objects mathematically, but whether these representations outperform or underperform matched spectral maps for those objects is not determined. Report equal-input and trivial-case counts.
4. AI ground truth? Public independent aerial/lidar-interpreted wetland inventory, with boundary and omission uncertainty documented; no claim of exact ground truth or legal delineation.
5. Executable? Public provider-pays AEF outputs are confirmed; reference data and matched Sentinel inputs require pilot.
6. Reader change? If small objects survive, cheaper embedding workflows gain support; if not, conservation map acceptance needs object-based checks and targeted validation despite clean appearance.
Constraint origin: no. Retain finalist; priority is evidence quality, not appearance.

# Topic divergence (in progress)

## Technology sources and scope of novelty

These are capability changes made practical around 2024 onward, not claims that the underlying tasks were impossible before. Dates refer to public research/tools, not commercial deployment. Sources were searched on 2026-10-27. Feasibility below is provisional; no candidate is yet selected.

| ID | Subfield and technology | Newly practical capability, speed/cost change, removed constraint (without technology names) | Source and date evidence | Local/public access |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Scientific machine learning: diffusion-based global ensemble weather prediction | Sample coherent global future weather trajectories with learned uncertainty; many scenarios in minutes rather than numerical integrations; reduced supercomputer dependence | Price et al., *Probabilistic weather forecasting with machine learning*, Nature 2024, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9 | Running full model locally unlikely; published forecasts/hindcasts require access pilot. Prior preprint appeared Dec 2023; practical public model 2024. |
| T2 | Geospatial representation learning: multimodal embedding fields | Reuse annual 10 m representations of the terrestrial surface across domains; inexpensive local supervised mapping after planetary preprocessing; reduced task-specific satellite processing | Brown et al., *AlphaEarth Foundations: An embedding field model for accurate and efficient global mapping from sparse label data*, 2025, arXiv:2507.22291 | Published CC BY 4.0 outputs on provider-pays GCS permit local range reads; no model execution needed. |
| T3 | Geometric computer vision: dense unconstrained stereo reconstruction | Infer dense geometry from uncalibrated, unposed image pairs; removes mandatory camera calibration and correspondence pipeline; much less capture preparation | Wang et al., *DUSt3R: Geometric 3D Vision Made Easy*, CVPR 2024, arXiv:2312.14132 | Public code/weights; local inference plausibly feasible on selected images. Dec 2023 preprint means borderline date, recorded explicitly. |
| T4 | Probabilistic sequence learning: zero-shot time-series foundation forecasting | Obtain predictive distributions on a new numerical series without fitting a domain model; repeated deployment becomes cheap; removes per-series training dependence | Ansari et al., *Chronos: Learning the Language of Time Series*, 2024, arXiv:2403.07815 | Small open models can run locally. Previously trained benchmark overlap needs checking. |
| T5 | Document perception: unified end-to-end optical decoding | Decode tables, ordinary text, formulae and other structured optical signals into machine-readable form with one small model; reduces bespoke OCR pipelines; removes document-type-specific parsers | Wei et al., *General OCR Theory: Towards OCR-2.0 via a Unified End-to-end Model*, 2024, arXiv:2409.01704 | Open 580M model; local feasibility plausible. OCR itself is old; only unified structured decoding qualifies. |
| T6 | Geometric deep learning for biomolecules: joint all-atom interaction prediction | Model heterogeneous molecular complexes together; reduces separate protein/ligand modeling; removes single-molecule-class input restriction | Abramson et al., *Accurate structure prediction of biomolecular interactions with AlphaFold 3*, 2024, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07487-w | Full inference likely infeasible locally; published safe environmental benchmark outputs may be usable. No pathogen or harmful design work considered. |

No idea here was generated from storage, time or record-keeping limits. These constraints will assess feasibility later. T1 and T3 have late-2023 preprints; their 2024 public practical capabilities are the basis for inclusion, not the publication year alone.

## Twelve question candidates generated from those combinations

Types: discovery/measurement (D), evaluated method (M), evidence dataset (B), reproduction/refutation (R). Counts: D=4, M=4, B=2, R=2. Each technology contributes two; no field contributes more than two. This table precedes the detailed screening and final selection.

| ID | Question; intended decision | Capability, data and necessity | Method/type |
|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | For heat-protection planning, does preserving ensemble temporal dependence improve prediction of sustained hot nights after matching marginal calibration? Heat officers would decide whether a calibrated per-day probability feed is sufficient for multi-day activation. | T1; public ensemble trajectories and reanalysis. Cheap coherent scenarios make wide matched-marginal evaluation possible; generic weather forecasts existed earlier. | Contrast intact trajectories, shuffled marginals and observational dependence reconstruction; D |
| C02 | Does an ensemble forecast selected for pointwise wind skill also support adequate buffers during prolonged regional wind droughts? Grid planners would decide whether selecting by usual forecast scores is adequate for reserve scheduling. | T1; public weather ensembles and reanalysis, public wind power-curve assumptions.

I treat the stated limits—no health outcomes, no farmer-intent inference, and no claim to identify the disaster’s true causal effect—as deliberate boundaries, not mistakes. The question is whether useful conclusions remain inside those boundaries. References below were confirmed by web search, including 2025–2026 work; cross-field analogues are distinguished from direct predecessors.

## C01 — Spatial dependence and shared surge capacity

- **The most serious weakness — (1) Effect on readers, with (6) question size.**  
  The unresolved issue is **which forecast-publication decision the experiment would change**. For a fixed portfolio and exceedance definition, the count distribution already preserves every capacity-exceedance probability; for one fixed capacity, publishing that tail probability requires just one number. Consequently, “one aggregate moment is more economical than a full pair matrix” is not enough: the practical competitor is the count distribution already included in the design.

  The study could change a provider’s choice of information product, but it cannot establish that a practitioner should activate capacity—that is explicitly outside its ensemble-information-retention target. Unless there is a concrete constraint preventing publication of the relevant count probabilities, the remaining result risks being a small comparison of summaries.

  On **unpredictability**, tighter bounds from nested information sets are guaranteed by construction; the genuinely empirical result is how often tightening crosses a relevant decision threshold. On **generality and realism**, that frequency depends on portfolio geometry, event definition and thresholds. A direct full-joint LP also has \(2^n\) states, creating a risk that the 12-hour budget restricts evaluation to unusually small portfolios.

- **Prior work that seems close**
  - András Prékopa, *Sharp Bounds on Probabilities Using Linear Programming* (1990), [URL](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.38.2.227). Classic moment-based bounds for exactly/at least \(r\) events; the same mathematical problem appears in operations research and **\(r\)-out-of-\(n\) system reliability**. ([pubsonline.informs.org](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.38.2.227?utm_source=openai))
  - Roman Schefzik, Thordis L. Thorarinsdottir and Tilmann Gneiting, *Uncertainty Quantification in Complex Simulation Models Using Ensemble Copula Coupling* (2013), [URL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149). Establishes the dependence-preservation problem and reconstruction framework. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149?utm_source=openai))
  - Arjun Ramachandra and Karthik Natarajan, *Tight Probability Bounds with Pairwise Independence* (2023), [URL](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1408294). Close probability-bounding machinery, although pairwise independence is a special restriction, not arbitrary observed pairwise dependence. ([epubs.siam.org](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/21M1408294?utm_source=openai))
  - Mylne et al., *Classification of Use Cases for Ensemble Weather Forecasts* (2025), [URL](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-24-0183.1). Already connects information-product design to decisions requiring coherent scenarios rather than marginal summaries. ([journals.ametsoc.org](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/bams/aop/BAMS-D-24-0183.1/BAMS-D-24-0183.1.xml?utm_source=openai))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**  
  The scalar moment certifies many more decisions than marginals alone, but a compact count distribution certifies all of them at negligible additional delivery cost. Alternatively, almost all apparent successes occur far from the action threshold. Researchers learn the performance of a particular compression scheme, while providers have little reason to adopt it.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**  
  Make this a **constrained publication-choice evaluation**: specify the portfolios, thresholds and delivery constraint faced by a provider, then compare the additional decision information preserved per transmitted quantity—including count probabilities, not merely the pair matrix. Concentrate inference on representative, nontrivial decisions near the action boundary. This keeps the existing question but gives the certification rates an operational interpretation.

## C02 — Successive forecast confirmation and useful lead time

- **The most serious weakness — (3) Realism.**  
  **Matching false-alarm frequency is not necessarily matching operational burden.** Two policies can have equal false-alarm rates but different activation durations, cancellations, repeated mobilizations and missed preparation deadlines. The proposed resource-budget analysis helps only if these quantities are represented in the resource model. Otherwise, confirmation can look wasteful because the costs it is intended to avoid have been omitted.

  The concrete reader decision is whether to retain, relax or remove confirmation for a particular protective action. An unspecified protection-cost sweep may not support that judgment. Likewise, lead time calculated only among successfully warned events can obscure events a policy fails to protect.

  On **novelty and unpredictability**, an ideal stopping policy weakly outperforming an included confirmation rule under its own correctly specified objective is unsurprising; attainable held-out improvement is not. Calling a fitted policy “optimal” also risks overstating what can be learned from a small number of independent heat episodes. Generalization will depend on update cadence, calibration, episode definition and action deadlines—not just forecast accuracy.

- **Prior work that seems close**
  - Abraham Wald, *Sequential Tests of Statistical Hypotheses* (1945), [URL](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177731118). The classic **sequential evidence-accumulation** analogue; it is not a directly applicable solution for correlated forecast revisions. ([cir.nii.ac.jp](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1363107371158888448?utm_source=openai))
  - Stephen Jewson, Sebastian Scher and Gabriele Messori, *Decide Now or Wait for the Next Forecast? Testing a Decision Framework Using Real Forecasts and Observations* (2021), [URL](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-20-0392.1). Directly addresses the act-now-versus-wait question through an extended cost–loss model. ([journals.ametsoc.org](https://journals.ametsoc.org/abstract/journals/mwre/149/5/MWR-D-20-0392.1.xml?utm_source=openai))
  - Jewson, Scher and Messori, *Communicating Properties of Changes in Lagged Weather Forecasts* (2022), [URL](https://doi.org/10.1175/WAF-D-21-0086.1). Studies forecast-change information using temperature forecasts and observations. ([journals.ametsoc.org](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/37/1/WAF-D-21-0086.1.xml?utm_source=openai))
  - Erica Thompson, *Anticipating Crises Due to Extreme Heat in Pakistan* (2018), [URL](https://www.ericathompson.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Anticipating-crises-due-to-extreme-heat-in-Pakistan-final-report.pdf). An operationally close report proposing an early anticipatory alert followed by later forecast confirmation—not simply repeated binary alarms. ([ericathompson.co.uk](https://www.ericathompson.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Anticipating-crises-due-to-extreme-heat-in-Pakistan-final-report.pdf?utm_source=openai))
  - David S. Richardson et al., *Skill and Consistency of ECMWF Forecasts of Atlantic Tropical Cyclone Genesis* (2025), [URL](https://doi.org/10.1175/WAF-D-24-0115.1). Demonstrates that revision consistency, forecast skill and event definitions must be separated; it does not settle the heat-policy comparison. ([journals.ametsoc.org](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/40/5/WAF-D-24-0115.1.xml?utm_source=openai))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**  
  Confirmation loses one forecast cycle under a model where early action is always beneficial and cancellation is free. The learned policy reduces modeled expense, but the organization’s actual constraint is staffing changes or maintaining an activation for several days. No practitioner has grounds to change its protocol; the study mostly illustrates the assumed loss function.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**  
  Evaluate **one complete, episode-based protective-action protocol** with a documented preparation deadline and activation-duration or cancellation costs. Match total burden over entire episodes, count missed deadlines as well as warning lead time, and estimate uncertainty by episode rather than initialization. This tests when confirmation is genuinely dominated, rather than merely slower.

## C05 — Temporary crop interruption versus persistent mapped abandonment

- **The most serious weakness — (1) Effect on readers, arising from (3) endpoint realism.**  
  The central gap is **between predicting a map-defined category and providing earlier, decision-relevant evidence of persistent interruption**. Excluding future labels is necessary, but the outline still needs a fixed prediction date, persistence horizon and accounting for when annual embeddings actually become available.

  Without those, a successful result could mean only that embeddings reproduce—or anticipate—the behavior of a particular crop-map pipeline. Spatial and temporal holdouts do not remove shared measurement errors between imagery-derived predictors and imagery-derived labels. The absence of intent labels is not fatal to the stated endpoint; it does, however, limit the conclusions to that endpoint.

  Incremental performance over label histories is genuinely unpredictable. Nevertheless, randomly hiding labels where public annual histories already exist could create an artificial label-scarcity benefit. Transfer across farming systems, crop-map taxonomies and persistent classification errors is also more consequential than transfer between nearby spatial blocks. The reader judgment that needs to change is whether embeddings justify **earlier follow-up or monitoring expenditure**, not simply which feature set scores better.

Already distinguishes annual activity, persistent fallow and recultivation, and tests sensitivity to definitions. ([pages.cms.hu-berlin.de](https://pages.cms.hu-berlin.de/biogeo/website/publication/2015-01-01_mapping_farmland_aba/?utm_source=openai))
  - Pontus Olofsson et al., *Good Practices for Estimating Area and Assessing Accuracy of Land Change* (2014), [URL](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0034425714000704). The standard validation framework emphasizes reference quality, sampling design and class-specific uncertainty—not agreement with another map alone. ([sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0034425714000704?utm_source=openai))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**  
  Multi-year embeddings improve classification of future persistent non-crop labels, but gains occur mainly for obvious conversions or stable map errors. Temporary fallow and crop rotation remain difficult, and the gain arrives no earlier than existing histories would support a useful decision. The result changes a mapping implementation, not understanding of interruption or monitoring priorities.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**  
  Frame the existing comparison as **early identification at a fixed information cutoff**: among newly interrupted parcels, ask whether embeddings improve prediction of a specified later persistence endpoint over all label history available at that cutoff. Preserve realistic class prevalence and temporary-interruption cases. The useful output becomes how much earlier—and with what false-positive burden—persistent mapped interruption can be identified.

## C06 — Representation-based forest recovery ordering

- **The most serious weakness — (1) Effect on readers, through (3) measurement validity.**  
  **Agreement between proxies may not establish that the embedding adds a useful recovery ordering.** Even after controlling pre-disturbance cover, agreement could be driven by time since disturbance, initial damage severity or shared imagery rather than distinguishing unusually slow and fast recovery among comparable sites.

  This criticism applies even to the deliberately limited, proxy-only claim: the issue is not merely that canopy products fail to measure “true ecology.” It is whether correspondence with those products warrants using representation distance for monitoring or prioritization. Spatial holdouts do not establish independence of measurement errors.

  On **generality**, raw embedding distance is representation-specific; a result for one geometry does not automatically validate a general recovery concept. On **realism**, requiring both pre-disturbance embeddings and follow-up restricts usable cohorts; long government disturbance histories do not create missing historical embeddings. On **novelty and question size**, reproducing a broad fire-versus-harvest ordering is weaker than showing incremental discrimination within comparable recovery cohorts.

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**  
  Embedding distance reproduces the average ordering of disturbance groups and correlates with canopy return, but elapsed time and a conventional spectral metric perform equally well. It cannot identify slow-recovering sites within a cohort. Practitioners gain another visualization without a reason to change monitoring priorities.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**  
  Test **incremental within-cohort ranking validity**: within comparable disturbance age, baseline condition and severity, determine whether representation distance preserves reference-product ordering better than simple age and spectral baselines. Distinguish structural-reference agreement from spectral-reference agreement. This would identify a defensible boundary for using the metric, rather than merely confirming broad recovery trajectories.

## C07 — Disaster-year representations and matching bias

- **The most serious weakness — (4) Novelty and (5) unpredictability.**  
  The general warning and first-line remedy are already established: **adjusting for treatment-affected information can bias effect estimates; use defensibly pre-treatment covariates**. The remaining contribution must therefore be a non-obvious result about when an annual-window guard helps enough to justify the information it discards.

  Known-effect constructed outcomes can test mechanisms, but their bias magnitudes depend on the chosen construction. An observational change in estimated contrast cannot validate those magnitudes. Moreover, post-treatment adjustment need not produce attenuation specifically; bias direction depends on the causal structure. The candidate correctly acknowledges that attenuation alone is insufficient, but that leaves “the contrast gets smaller” with little independent evidential value. ([academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssa/article/147/5/656/7106019?searchresult=1&utm_source=openai))

  For readers, the actionable change is a concrete rule for selecting representation windows—not renewed awareness of bad controls. A calendar-year label is also less informative than the actual observation-support window. The general principle is durable; a numerical attenuation result for one disaster, embedding version and matching procedure is not automatically general.

- **Prior work that seems close**
  - Paul R. Rosenbaum, *The Consequences of Adjustment for a Concomitant Variable That Has Been Affected by the Treatment* (1984), [URL](https://doi.org/10.2307/2981697). The classic direct predecessor, covering observational adjustment and matched sampling. This is the established **bad-control/post-treatment-adjustment** problem under another name. ([academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssa/article/147/5/656/7106019?searchresult=1&utm_source=openai))
  - Connor T. Jerzak, Fredrik Johansson and Adel Daoud, *Estimating Causal Effects Under Image Confounding Bias with an Application to Poverty in Africa* (2022), [URL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06410). Establishes assumptions and simulation-based evaluation for image-derived confounding adjustment. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06410))
  - Adel Daoud, Richard Johansson and Connor T. Jerzak, *Detecting and Mitigating Treatment Leakage in Text-Based Causal Inference: Distillation and Sensitivity Analysis* (2026 arXiv listing; submitted December 30, 2025), [URL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02400). A particularly close rich-representation analogue, explicitly examining the tradeoff between leakage removal and confounder retention. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02400v1))
  - Brown et al., *AlphaEarth Foundations* (2025; full title above), [URL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22291). Distinguishes input-support intervals from embedding-summary intervals, providing the relevant provenance distinction for a temporal guard. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22291v2))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**  
  Constructed outcomes show bias when matching incorporates disaster consequences, and prior-year matching removes it. The real case changes in the same direction, but its true effect remains unknown. Researchers already avoiding post-treatment controls change no practice, while others receive a useful tutorial rather than new evidence about when a guard is reliable.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**  
  Evaluate the guard as a **leakage-versus-confounding-control tradeoff tied to actual event timing and observation support**. Use the existing representations and several plausible known-effect constructions—including cases where concurrent information is harmless or more informative—and measure bias alongside overlap and pre-event balance. The contribution would be conditions under which the guard is beneficial, unnecessarily conservative or insufficient, rather than another demonstration that post-treatment adjustment can fail.

# Wetland reference screening: main protocol, version 1

This version is fixed after topic selection and before collection of main-study data. The Git commit and observed fixing time are recorded in events.jsonl. No main outcomes have been observed. Changes require an appended deviation or a new version; outcome-dependent changes are exploratory.

## Question, use and estimand

Does adding annual satellite embedding distances improve wetland reference screening beyond public terrain and surface-water covariate matching, at fixed screening coverage, when vegetation subclass and geographic setting are controlled?

The endpoint is exact agreement with the **published National Wetlands Inventory (NWI) average water-regime category**, not current hydroperiod, restoration success or functional equivalence. This is a label-masked retrospective screening diagnostic: training wetlands have labels; neither query nor candidate reference labels are method inputs. If both labels are already known, direct regime filtering suffices and this workflow has no demonstrated advantage. Users are wetland reference-assessment researchers and practitioners deciding whether latent-space similarity merits further investigation alongside explicit hydrological evidence. No human or field benefit is measured.

Primary hypothesis: an augmented pair-compatibility learner using hydrological covariates plus annual embedding cosine distance reduces category mismatch at 50% screening coverage relative to **both** a hydrological-only pair learner and a hydrological-only regime decoder. An improvement confined to a weaker comparator will not support incremental-value language. A meaningful descriptive improvement is at least 0.02 absolute mismatch probability; report estimates and uncertainty regardless of direction. The two representations are separate tests of scope, not independent data replications. Negative outcomes, sparse settings and acquisition failures remain results. No claim of universal encoder behavior.

These settings span several wetland landscapes but are not a probability sample of the US. All are retained in the accounting, including empty or failed windows. ## Features and quality checks

For each embedding, take the 3×3 native-grid pixel centres surrounding the interior point, retaining centres inside its polygon and finite/unmasked vectors. Require >=5 valid pixels for that representation, average decoded vectors and unit-normalize the mean. Save all nine raw values/masks and scales. Primary complete-case panel requires terrain, occurrence and recurrence plus both embeddings; if the second representation fails technically, report separate AEF panel and the TESSERA failure rather than dropping settings selectively. Also report each representation's own coverage. Sensitivities: centre pixel only and minimum interior distance 30m, without changing the primary set.

Hydrological covariates (fixed before label modelling): mean and standard deviation of elevation, slope magnitude (gradient at 30m), topographic-position index relative to 90m and 510m square neighbourhoods, all summarized in 90m around point; occurrence, recurrence and seasonality centre/90m mean and 90m standard deviation; occurrence/recurrence 510m mean; fraction of occurrence>0 and occurrence>=75 in 90m and 510m; log(1+distance in metres to occurrence>=75), capped 2km; log polygon area. Export raster buffers >=2.1km beyond window. Invalid/no-data water pixels are missing rather than zero; valid 0 water pixels are zero. Missing optional seasonal input omitted consistently for all settings. Missing individual covariates are median-imputed using training data only, with missingness indicators. If essential terrain/occurrence/recurrence are absent, retain acquisition failure accounting and exclude that window from matched comparisons.

Use a fixed hash sample of 36 polygons (three/window, or all if fewer) to audit original code parsing, allowed categories, projection/point-in-polygon, mask orientation, date join and patch coverage against saved inputs and official definitions. This is a self-check, not field validation or independent annotation. Test geographical forward/inverse transforms and known raster bounds. Compare water values with documented range, DEM with numerical service queries, and embedding norms/quantization/masks; these are validity checks, not model-effect results.

## Spatial design and candidate pools

Split each square into 4×4 blocks of 4km. Fixed fold = `(block_x + 2*block_y) mod 4`. Four rotations: query fold r, reference fold (r+1) mod4, training the remaining two. Polygons are never shared across roles in a rotation. Use selected interior coordinates; no neighbouring parts of the same OBJECTID cross roles. Exact train/query/ref IDs and counts are saved. Reuse across rotations is acknowledged; aggregate by setting before uncertainty estimation. No random-polygon split is the primary evaluation.

A query's candidates are sampled wetlands in the same window and exact vegetation subclass, in the reference role, at least **1km** from the query. Training pairs likewise same-window/same-subclass and >=1km apart, among training-role polygons. A missing pool produces no screening opportunity, is counted and excluded from conditional mismatch denominators. Also count: one-category pools; pools lacking the query category (unmatchable); pools with >=2 categories and containing the query category (nontrivial matchable); pools with all same/different potential labels; number and distance of candidates. This reveals floors/ceilings without pretending labels are available to the method.

All primary methods face identical queries and pools. Each chooses the maximum predicted compatibility or minimum distance, ties by reference OBJECTID. Then screen the top **50%** of nonempty query pools separately in each window/rotation using that method's confidence; number retained is floor(n/2), ties by query OBJECTID. These are conditional fixed-budget diagnostics, not prospective calibrated acceptability guarantees. Primary risk = category mismatch among screened queries, region-macro averaged after pooling each region's four rotations. Also report pooled risk, full coverage and coverage 20%, 80%; risk–coverage curves. Conditional nontrivial-matchable results use the already-screened set (no label-dependent rescreening) and report achieved conditional coverage.

## Comparisons and learning, fixed choices

1. Geographic nearest reference (confidence negative distance); analytical random-reference expected mismatch per query; label-aware oracle lower bound. The latter two are diagnostics, not deployable methods.
2. Explicit hydrological Euclidean distance: training median/IQR scales, symmetric standardized L2, missing indicators, excluding region/vegetation constants. Confidence negative nearest distance.
3. **Hydrology pair learner**: RandomForestClassifier, 200 trees, max_depth=12, min_samples_leaf=20, max_features=0.7, n_jobs=2, random_state=18018+r. Features are symmetric mean and absolute difference of imputed/scaled hydrological values plus missing indicators, log(1+geographic distance), one-hot vegetation and region. Target: exact regime agreement. Per training wetland, sample up to 30 other admissible training wetlands using fixed RNG 18018+r, deduplicate unordered pairs; do not balance by outcomes. This is the primary learned matching comparator.
4. **Hydrology decoder**: same forest settings except min_samples_leaf=10, multiclass A/C/F from individual hydrological covariates, vegetation and region. Reference compatibility is the dot product of query and candidate predicted category probabilities. Training labels only; query/reference labels withheld. This is a distinct strong comparator against an artificially weak pair model.
5. Augmented pair learner: identical to (3), append cosine distance for AEF, or TESSERA, or both. No tuning using query outcomes. Primary contrasts are each single representation vs (3) and (4). Combined representation is a planned secondary comparison.
6. Raw cosine nearest neighbour for each representation. Centre and whiten representations using training data only (ridge covariance eigenvalues floored at 1% of largest), cosine nearest neighbour, planned secondary. These separate default metric behavior from trained matching.
7. Information diagnostic: individual AEF or TESSERA full-vector forest decoders, and hydrological+vector decoder (same classifier settings), scored for balanced accuracy and log loss on query folds. These test recoverability with one specified learner; failure does not prove information absent. Decoder-pair compatibility also scored as a planned strong representation-aware alternative.

All transforms are fit using the rotation's training wetlands only. No test-set hyperparameter search. Print/save feature definitions, fit seeds, numbers of pairs and class distributions. Use identical core data for paired contrasts. No external models, AI judges or human labels are required.

## Analysis, repetitions and uncertainty

The four prescribed spatial rotations exhaust the role scheme; they are not four independent replications. Main tables show region-macro risk at coverage .5, paired absolute differences versus both hydrological learners, and counts/floors above. Compute 95% percentile bootstrap intervals by resampling **regions**, 10,000 resamples, seed 18018, using paired method estimates. With <4 analysable regions, omit inferential interval and present descriptive results. With >=4, still describe these as convenience-panel uncertainty, not nationally representative inference. Report all planned contrasts without selecting the best representation for the abstract. No significance claims from uncorrected many-comparison p-values; no p-values are planned.

Planned boundary conditions: geographic buffer 0m and 3km (1km primary); minimum 30m polygon interior margin; small (<=1ha) vs larger polygons; vegetation subclass; image-date groups; within-region z-scoring vs global training scaling; recent labels (>=2020); matchable/nontrivial pools as defined above; matched own-representation versus common panel. Recompute selection and state denominators where a restriction changes the candidate pool. AEF years **2023 and 2021** on the same points, and TESSERA 2023/2021 if accessible, test stability of inventory agreement, not annual hydrological validation. No temporal variation claim will be made from fixed inventory labels alone. Obtain alternate-year data only after initial extraction succeeds and adequate time/storage remain.

Additional independently trained representations, stronger learned metrics, national prevalence, current field hydrology and actual practitioner decisions are outside the primary design. Further analyses after observing main results must be labelled exploratory and get a dated protocol amendment before execution.

## Literature range and stopping criteria

Existing search range: public scholarly/official sources searched with wetland reference condition, hydrogeomorphic classification, hydrological analogue/donor selection, satellite embeddings wetland hydrology, temporal representation consistency and ecological restoration similarity; 2025+ searches and closest full-text comparisons are in prior-work-candidates.md and sources.jsonl. Repeat the close search during self-check, record full-text passage checks for evidence citations, and distinguish abstracts/pointers. Do not assert novelty.

Acquisition stops after the fixed panel, >=2 bounded attempts per failed source (at most 3 if a documented transport correction is justified), or a resource/deadline limit. A technical failure does not authorize replacement sites. Numerical analysis runs once under this design then needed bug corrections/re-runs are recorded. Each process <=1800s, at most two; no long new experiments during final hour. Storage warning at 8GiB, stop new large acquisitions at 9GiB; total must stay <10GiB. Deadline 2026-10-28T02:23:30Z, no extensions currently. If >=2h remain after planned experiments, prioritize alternative explanations and stronger baselines/realistic boundary conditions within this question, with amendments before added work. No field visits or experiments on human participants. All failures and partial results are submitted.

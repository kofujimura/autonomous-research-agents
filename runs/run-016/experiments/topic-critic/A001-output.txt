I treat the proposed safeguards—matched marginals, spatial separation, limited-label corrections, and explicit proxy disclaimers—as part of each design, not as missing features. The references below were confirmed by web search, including 2025–2026 work available by **October 26, 2026**. Preprints are identified.

## Q01 — Forecast-aware heat-response capacity sharing

- **The most serious weakness — (3) Realism: simultaneous heat does not identify simultaneous donor unavailability.**

  The temperature proxy is explicitly acknowledged, but the central operational quantity remains unobserved: **deployable surplus capacity after a donor meets its own needs**. A region exceeding a temperature threshold might still have spare resources; another might be unable to lend despite remaining below it. The proposal also needs to identify what is reservable and transferable—not simply “heat-response capacity.”

  Consequently, the replay could establish that joint forecasts predict simultaneous temperature exceedances without establishing when usual partners cannot help. The reader judgment worth changing **(1)** is: *“At this lead time and price, should we reserve an alternative source rather than rely on our usual partners?”* That requires a credible connection between weather, retained capacity, and feasible transfers. Existing heat-policy work already recognizes synchronized emergencies and proposes forecast-triggered mutual aid; merely illustrating that concern would add little. ([kleinmanenergy.upenn.edu](https://kleinmanenergy.upenn.edu/research/publications/rethinking-extreme-heat-and-urban-resilience-through-scales-connectivity/))

  Within 12 hours, access to suitable surface-ensemble archives is also a go/no-go condition. The acknowledged upper-air-only archive cannot establish the proposed result.

- **Prior work that seems close**

  - **Gary D. Eppen (1979), [“Note—Effects of Centralization on Expected Costs in a Multi-Location Newsboy Problem”](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.25.5.498).** The classic operations-research analogue: the benefit of pooling depends on demand correlation. Thus, “correlated needs undermine geographic pooling” is already established **(4–5)**. ([pubsonline.informs.org](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.25.5.498?utm_source=openai))
  - **Roman Schefzik, Thordis L. Thorarinsdottir, and Tilmann Gneiting (2013), [“Uncertainty Quantification in Complex Simulation Models Using Ensemble Copula Coupling”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149).** Establishes marginal calibration followed by dependence-preserving rearrangement, including connections to the Schaake shuffle. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149))
  - **Ilan Price et al. (2025), [“Probabilistic weather forecasting with machine learning”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9).** Already evaluates spatially aggregated forecast quantities and regional wind power; the general usefulness of coherent joint forecasts is not the remaining novelty. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9?utm_source=openai))
  - **Mohammad Kian Golkar, Luciano Alves de Oliveira, and Mohamad Alipour (2026, preprint), [“Forecast lead time and the timing of crew deployment for weather-driven power outages using AI weather models”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20918).** Directly overlaps in forecast-conditioned staging and the trade-off between earlier action and crew availability, although not in forecasting joint donor unavailability. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20918))
  - **Xueke Li, Michael E. Mann, Shannon Christiansen, and Heather Kostick (2026), [“Rethinking Extreme Heat and Urban Resilience through Scales Connectivity”](https://kleinmanenergy.upenn.edu/research/publications/rethinking-extreme-heat-and-urban-resilience-through-scales-connectivity/).** A particularly close policy antecedent: explicitly proposes heat-specific mutual aid for jurisdictions forecast to be affected simultaneously. It is not, however, an evaluation of your reservation policy. ([kleinmanenergy.upenn.edu](https://kleinmanenergy.upenn.edu/research/publications/rethinking-extreme-heat-and-urban-resilience-through-scales-connectivity/))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**

  Joint forecasts beat independence and fixed geographic pools, but not the **already planned historical-dependence comparator**. Alternatively, they identify joint shortages accurately, yet every feasible donor is also constrained, leaving no better action. A positive simulated saving confined to one assumed capacity ratio, transfer radius, or forecast archive would be a small policy-model result **(2, 6)**. The predictable pooling mechanism would have been reconfirmed; the actionable incremental forecast value would remain unresolved **(5)**.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**

  Make the existing replay a **break-even analysis for one documented transferable resource and mutual-aid network**. Use explicit, bounded assumptions about local retention and travel time, and ask whether the reservation recommendation survives those bounds against historical-dependence forecasts. This preserves the question while making the result about a defensible decision rather than a temperature-defined fictional shortage.

## Q02 — Recovery-aware release of reserves during low-wind episodes

- **The most serious weakness — (3) Realism: the reserve decision is insufficiently specified to establish why recovery-time information matters.**

  “Reserve release” could mean releasing a fuel commitment, ending demand-response readiness, discharging storage, or decommitting generation. Those are different intertemporal decisions. If losses are simply additive daily holding and shortage penalties, with no restart costs, irreversible release, or other interday constraints, daily marginal forecasts can already be sufficient. The experiment must establish the actual source of path dependence rather than introduce it only through the proposed warning rule.

  Likewise, crossing a wind threshold does not necessarily mean an energy deficit has ended. Hydrological drought methods have already been transferred to wind droughts specifically to avoid treating brief threshold recoveries as separate episodes and to represent accumulated deficits. ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13333602/?utm_source=openai))

  The judgment worth changing **(1)** is therefore not “duration matters,” but *“Under these commitment constraints, when can this resource safely be released?”* Correct wind-height matching is necessary, but does not by itself make that decision realistic.

- **Prior work that seems close**

  - **Pierre Pinson, Henrik Madsen, Henrik Aalborg Nielsen, Georgios Papaefthymiou, and Bernd Klöckl (2009), [“From probabilistic forecasts to statistical scenarios of short-term wind power production”](https://doi.org/10.1002/we.284).** A standard antecedent for reconstructing temporal scenarios from per-horizon forecasts. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1002/WE.284?utm_source=openai))
  - **Roman Schefzik, Thordis L. Thorarinsdottir, and Tilmann Gneiting (2013), [“Uncertainty Quantification in Complex Simulation Models Using Ensemble Copula Coupling”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149).** The proposed dependence-preserving machinery is established, not a new forecasting principle. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149))
  - **P. Potisomporn, T. A. A. Adcock, and C. R. Vogel (2024; online 2023), [“Extreme value analysis of wind droughts in Great Britain”](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A87bfd681-1546-49fb-ad2e-3e923ec9a32b).** Uses hydrological event-pooling procedures and the sequent peak algorithm to characterize wind droughts, including interrupted low-generation episodes. This is a close connection under another field’s terminology. ([ora.ox.ac.uk](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A87bfd681-1546-49fb-ad2e-3e923ec9a32b?utm_source=openai))
  - **Richard Laugesen, Mark Thyer, David McInerney, and Dmitri Kavetski (2023), [“Flexible forecast value metric suitable for a wide range of decisions: application using probabilistic subseasonal streamflow forecasts”](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-27-873-2023).** Establishes decision-specific utility evaluation beyond simple threshold decisions. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-27-873-2023))
  - **Meng Qu et al. (2025), [“Prolonged wind droughts in a warming climate threaten global wind power security”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41558-025-02387-x).** Recent evidence that duration is a consequential target, but not evidence about forecast-conditioned reserve release. ([nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-025-02387-x?utm_source=openai))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**

  The recovery-aware rule beats a memoryless daily threshold but ties a tuned rule requiring several reassuring updates before release. The improvement would then be an unsurprising benefit of avoiding repeated switching **(4–5)**, not evidence that coherent forecast trajectories provide valuable recovery information. Results tied to one threshold and unspecified reserve would also transfer poorly across electricity systems **(2)** and amount to a small warning-rule comparison **(6)**. Matching reserve-days helps, but does not eliminate this possibility.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**

  Specify **one intertemporal reserve contract**, including release and recommitment delay, and replay repeated forecast updates for it. Compare recovery forecasts with a tuned daily-probability rule using episode age and hysteresis—the existing historical-run comparator can serve this role. Evaluate premature release against the contract’s relevant sustained deficit, including episodes whose recovery lies beyond the forecast horizon.

## Q03 — Annual embeddings and ecological recovery after wildfire

- **The most serious weakness — (1) Effect on readers: the design cannot adjudicate the ecological claim in the headline.**

  The distinction between disturbance disappearance, pre-fire similarity, and biological recovery is correct. But explicitly disclaiming biological measurement leaves an inferential ceiling: **matched controls can improve interpretation of embedding movement without validating ecological recovery**.

  Agreement with spectral-index recovery is not an independent biological check. Recent synthesis documents that spectral recovery can reflect shrubs or grasses rather than conifer recovery, and that relationships between spectral and field-based recovery vary by method and location. ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10021-025-01029-9)) Thus, researchers may learn which representation-distance definition is less misleading, while practitioners still cannot change a restoration, replanting, or recovery-certification decision **(3)**.

  The remaining representation audit also has substantial overlap with the two 2026 preprints. That does not prove the exact matched-control statistic has already been tested, but it narrows the novel claim considerably **(4)**. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25731))

- **Prior work that seems close**

  - **Jovana Knezevic et al. (2026, preprint), [“Annual Earth-observation embeddings encode wildfire disturbance and support simplified burned area mapping”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25731).** Already compares TESSERA and AlphaEarth for fire encoding, mapping, timing, and transfer. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25731))
  - **Robin Young and Srinivasan Keshav (2026, preprint, submitted October 23), [“Earth embeddings are decomposable records of the land surface”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32617).** Directly examines ecological features in both products, disturbance timing, and differences in the visibility of rapidly recovering fires. It is not independent validation of biological recovery. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32617))
  - **Robert E. Kennedy, Zhiqiang Yang, and Warren B. Cohen (2010), [“Detecting trends in forest disturbance and recovery using yearly Landsat time series: 1. LandTrendr—Temporal segmentation algorithms”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2010.07.008).** The established trajectory-based comparator, rather than merely a single spectral-index threshold. ([research.fs.usda.gov](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/54160?utm_source=openai))
  - **A. J. Underwood (1994), [“On Beyond BACI: Sampling Designs that Might Reliably Detect Environmental Disturbances”](https://doi.org/10.2307/1942110).** The ecological before–after/control–impact tradition already addresses distinguishing disturbance responses from background temporal variation. Adding controls is sound design, not itself methodological novelty. ([esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2307/1942110?utm_source=openai))
  - **Sarah M. Smith-Tripp et al. (2026; published online December 8, 2025), [“Synthesizing Spectral and Field Observations of Post-fire Conifer Recovery in Dry Conifer Forests”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10021-025-01029-9).** Closely addresses the central interpretation problem: what spectral trajectories do and do not establish about recovery. ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10021-025-01029-9))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**

  The study shows that moving away from a fire-year vector is not equivalent to returning toward a pre-fire vector. That distinction is predictable without experiments **(5)**. If the empirical addition is only the fraction of pixels for which two current embedding products disagree, the result is both small **(6)** and representation-dependent **(2)**. Where the overlapping archive contains only a few post-fire years, it would also leave longer-term recovery outside the observable target.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**

  For a small set of fires with **existing independent canopy-structure observations**, test whether the matched-control rule reduces false declarations of structural recovery relative to the proposed embedding and spectral comparators. This remains the same recovery-validity question, but supplies an external criterion. It should still avoid claiming recovery of the whole ecosystem.

## Q04 — Crop-diversity change with frozen classifiers and limited-label adjustment

- **The most serious weakness — (3) Realism: the evaluation can certify agreement with a changing reference product rather than the direction of actual crop-diversity change.**

  The proposal correctly acknowledges that public crop maps are not field truth. Nevertheless, if both the limited-label correction and final evaluation use those maps, success can mean recovering the reference map’s proportions—including its changing errors. Spatially separated splits do not resolve this problem. Established area-estimation guidance specifically requires attention to reference-label quality and uncertainty, not just classification accuracy. ([nottingham-repository.worktribe.com](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/728216/good-practices-for-estimating-area-and-assessing-accuracy-of-land-change?utm_source=openai))

  The practitioner judgment worth changing **(1)** is: *“Can this small annual audit support reporting an increase or decrease, rather than commissioning fuller relabeling?”* Agreement with a raster alone cannot justify that judgment.

  There is also an estimand issue: **rotation is not necessarily landscape diversity change**. Every field in a two-crop landscape can rotate while the landscape remains exactly 50/50, with unchanged Shannon diversity. The study should not imply that tracking area-composition diversity also measures rotational diversity.

- **Prior work that seems close**

  - **Pontus Olofsson et al. (2014), [“Good practices for estimating area and assessing accuracy of land change”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2014.02.015).** Establishes probability-sampled, reference-based area estimation and uncertainty. The adjustment is an established monitoring method **(4)**. ([nottingham-repository.worktribe.com](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/728216/good-practices-for-estimating-area-and-assessing-accuracy-of-land-change?utm_source=openai))
  - **George Forman (2005), [“Counting Positives Accurately Despite Inaccurate Classification”](https://doi.org/10.1007/11564096_55).** The machine-learning field calls this *quantification*: estimating class prevalence rather than classifying each item correctly. ([citeseerx.ist.psu.edu](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=cce9f72ecf953605b61edc35a2962829c7fd976a&repid=rep1&type=pdf&utm_source=openai))
  - **Zachary Lipton, Yu-Xiang Wang, and Alexander Smola (2018), [“Detecting and Correcting for Label Shift with Black Box Predictors”](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/lipton18a.html).** Directly relevant to source-confusion adjustment, including its dependence on stable class-conditional distributions and an invertible confusion matrix. It should not be conflated with target-year design-based adjustment. ([proceedings.mlr.press](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/lipton18a?utm_source=openai))
  - **Madeline C. Lisaius, Srinivasan Keshav, Andrew Blake, and Clement Atzberger (2026, preprint), [“Embedding-based Crop Type Classification in the Groundnut Basin of Senegal”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16900).** Already evaluates the two representation families and temporal transfer, but not the proposed diversity-change estimand. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16900))
  - **Mehmet Ozgur Turkoglu, Selene Ledain, and Helge Aasen (2025, preprint), [“Model-Agnostic, Temperature-Informed Sampling Enhances Cross-Year Crop Mapping with Deep Learning”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12885).** Shows that cross-year transfer and uncertainty calibration are already studied through phenological alignment, not only through embedding choice. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12885))
  - **Zhengpeng Feng et al. (2025, revised 2026, preprint), [“TESSERA: Temporal Embeddings of Surface Spectra for Earth Representation and Analysis”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20380).** Already establishes label-efficient downstream learning; the remaining contribution must be monitoring validity rather than another label-efficiency benchmark. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20380))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**

  Target-year corrections improve proportions, but uncertainty still spans zero for most practically relevant diversity changes. Practitioners consequently cannot report a direction or reduce their audit burden. “Fresh labels help a stale classifier” is predictable **(5)**; the frequency and magnitude of consequential sign reversals are not. A winner among two embeddings on one crop-map product would be version- and geography-sensitive **(2)** and smaller than the monitoring question **(6)**.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**

  Make the endpoint **sign certification under a fixed audit budget**: report the minimum detectable change and when the procedure must abstain, including sensitivity to plausible class-specific reference-map errors. Use the existing landscape-years and estimators rather than adding more models. This would tell readers which change claims their monitoring budget can support.

## Q05 — Daily calibration versus delivery-interval demand reserves

- **The most serious weakness — (4–5) Novelty and unpredictability: the headline’s first question already has a mathematical answer.**

  Correct daily marginals do not determine the distribution of their sum. For example, two days can each have a 50% chance of demand zero or one, while their total is either always one or instead zero/two with equal probability. No experiment is needed to establish that daily calibration does not guarantee interval-reserve reliability.

  Moreover, dependence-aware lead-time demand distributions were already evaluated for intermittent inventories by Willemain and colleagues. Marginal-preserving dependence reconstruction is also established in ensemble forecasting. ([smartcorp.com](https://smartcorp.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/IJF_Bootstrap_paper_Smart_Software.pdf?utm_source=openai))

  The consequential unknown **(1)** is narrower: *“Does repairing an immediately usable pretrained forecast produce better reserve decisions than directly forecasting interval demand or using an established intermittent-demand model?”* Your proposed comparators recognize this; they must determine the conclusion. The unavailable clean holdout remains important: the acknowledged contaminated, zero-filled pilot cannot support that operational claim.

- **Prior work that seems close**

  - **J. D. Croston (1972), [“Forecasting and Stock Control for Intermittent Demands”](https://doi.org/10.1057/jors.1972.50).** The classic intermittent-demand baseline, separating demand occurrence and size and examining stock-control consequences. It is not, by itself, an adequate modern distributional comparator. ([tandfonline.com](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1057/jors.1972.50?utm_source=openai))
  - **Thomas R. Willemain, Charles N. Smart, and Henry F. Schwarz (2004), [“A new approach to forecasting intermittent demand for service parts inventories”](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0169-2070(03)00013-X).** Very close prior work: forecasts cumulative lead-time demand distributions and evaluates their calibration using industrial datasets. ([smartcorp.com](https://smartcorp.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/IJF_Bootstrap_paper_Smart_Software.pdf?utm_source=openai))
  - **Roman Schefzik, Thordis L. Thorarinsdottir, and Tilmann Gneiting (2013), [“Uncertainty Quantification in Complex Simulation Models Using Ensemble Copula Coupling”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149).** The same broad intervention—changing dependence while retaining calibrated marginal distributions—under weather-forecasting terminology. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149))
  - **Abdul Fatir Ansari et al. (2024), [“Chronos: Learning the Language of Time Series”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815).** Supplies the pretrained-model setting and explicitly distinguishes performance on training-corpus datasets from genuinely unseen datasets. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815))
  - **Insup Kim and Sangwha Song (2026), [“When Time-Series Foundation Models Work for Quick-Commerce Demand Forecasting”](https://doi.org/10.13106/jidb.2026.vol17.no3.89).** Already compares foundation models with intermittent-demand alternatives and finds suitability depends on demand pattern and objective. It does not establish joint interval-demand calibration. ([accesson.kr](https://www.accesson.kr/jidb/v.17/3/89/59736?utm_source=openai))

- **A scenario in which the value would be small even if it succeeds**

  Reordering improves one pretrained model’s summed-demand coverage but still loses to direct interval forecasting or the Markov bootstrap. That would be a useful model diagnostic, not a reason to change procurement **(1, 6)**, and its numerical advantage could disappear with a model revision **(2)**. The food-procurement interpretation would be especially weak if the data are spare-parts transactions or if apparent zero demand actually denotes missing records, closures, or unfulfilled requests **(3)**. Dependence repair cannot recover demand that was never observed.

- **A direction that broadens the question or brings it closer to reality**

  Center the existing experiment on **whether dependence repair remains worthwhile as the delivery interval changes**, using one genuinely held-out source with documented zero/missing-value semantics. Compare service–inventory trade-offs against direct interval forecasts and the Markov bootstrap, including the data needed to fit the repair. This turns a known calibration warning into a bounded, actionable procurement result without requiring a new forecasting architecture.

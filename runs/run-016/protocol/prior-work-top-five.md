## Q01 — forecast-conditioned shared heat-response capacity

Three closest works:
1. Golkar, Alves de Oliveira & Alipour (2026), *Forecast lead time and the timing of crew deployment for weather-driven power outages using AI weather models*, arXiv:2610.20918v1. Full text: sources/crew-deployment-2026.pdf/.txt, especially §§2.8–2.10 and 3.1–3.3. They compare ranked staging and early/late availability with observed outages. Proposed difference: estimate joint donor unavailability in the forecast rather than assume an exogenous late-availability ratio; heat response is a separate impact proxy.
2. Schefzik, Thorarinsdottir & Gneiting (2013), *Uncertainty Quantification in Complex Simulation Models Using Ensemble Copula Coupling*, DOI 10.1214/13-STS443, arXiv:1302.7149v2. Full text: sources/ecc-2013.pdf/.txt, §§3.4, 4, 5.1–5.4. Marginal calibration and rank reordering are established; they found strong pressure-dependence benefits but little temperature benefit between their three widely separated stations. Proposed difference: compare actual reserve decisions across simultaneous donor shortfalls, not present coherence as a new method or guaranteed improvement.
3. Price et al. (2025; online December 2024), *Probabilistic weather forecasting with machine learning*, DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9. Relevant article passages checked through publisher/DOI web retrieval (sources/search-top5-crossfield-1.json and search-data-prior-2.json); joint samples, cost-loss extremes, and wind-power aggregation are already evaluated. Proposed difference: conditional resource sharing rather than forecasting marginal extremes or aggregated power.

Standard/classic methods: cost-loss forecast value (Richardson 2000, *Skill and relative economic value of the ECMWF ensemble prediction system*, DOI 10.1002/qj.49712656313; related full 1998 technical memorandum saved); Schaake shuffle (Clark et al. 2004, *The Schaake Shuffle: A Method for Reconstructing Space–Time Variability in Forecasted Precipitation and Temperature Fields*, sources/schaake-shuffle-2004.pdf). Terms also searched: mutual assistance, correlated demand, donor availability, simultaneous heatwaves, reserve staging, empirical copula, decision value. Primary practice sources include official heat emergency plans returned in sources/novelty-refinement-1.json. These establish that mutual aid belongs to heat response, not that the proposed forecast rule helps.

Feasibility caveat: publicly accessible metadata demonstrate the available NeuralGCM ensemble lacks the surface variables required. WeatherNext Gen requires an access form; WeatherNext 3 raw ensemble has paid egress. Neither is accessed. Simply using IFS would abandon the claimed enabling recent capability; do not conceal that substitution.

## Q02 — recovery-aware low-wind reserve release

Three closest works:
1. Schefzik et al. (2013), title and full-text sections as above: established method for coherent temporal and spatial postprocessing. Proposed difference: evaluate release timing at matched reserve cost, not propose copula reordering itself.
2. Laugesen et al. (2023), *Flexible forecast value metric suitable for a wide range of decisions: application using probabilistic subseasonal streamflow forecasts*, DOI 10.5194/hess-27-873-2023. Full text: sources/flexible-value-2023.pdf/.txt, §§2.1–3.1, 4–5. It establishes why fixed probability thresholds can lose value and compares utility optimization in binary/continuous decisions. Proposed difference: wind-episode recovery/release, with fixed capacity-day comparisons so longer retention does not win mechanically.
3. Patlakas, Galanis, Diamantis & Kallos (2017), *Low wind speed events: persistence and frequency*, DOI 10.1002/we.2078. Search/abstract passages checked, including extreme-value analysis of duration/frequency; full PDF at the authors' university timed out and is not treated as full-text evidence. Proposed difference: forecast-conditioned release rather than climatological persistence.

Additional 2025+ search: *Prolonged wind droughts in a warming climate threaten global wind power security* (Nature Climate Change 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41558-025-02387-x), abstract/figure descriptions only; *Shallow and deep low-wind events in Europe: an ERA5-based event dataset (1979–2024)* (ESSD preprint 2026-483), abstract only. Both warn against treating a single near-surface level as turbine-height risk. Terms also searched: Dunkelflaute, low-wind persistence, wind drought duration, backup release, run length, cessation forecasting. Data caveat: NeuralGCM has upper-air winds only, so the proposed near-surface outcome cannot be silently replaced by pressure-level wind.

## Q03 — annual embeddings and ecological recovery

Three closest works:
1. Knezevic et al. (2026), *Annual Earth-observation embeddings encode wildfire disturbance and support simplified burned area mapping*, arXiv:2609.25731v1. Full text: sources/wildfire-embeddings-2026.pdf/.txt, §§2–3 and results. It already compares pre-, event-, and post-fire embeddings, burned/unburned controls, two representation families, transfer and timing. Proposed original difference was a recovery interpretation rather than detection, but the overlap is substantial.
2. *Earth embeddings are decomposable records of the land surface* (2026), arXiv:2610.32617v1. Full text: sources/decomposable-embeddings-2026.pdf/.txt, §§2.3–2.6 and Methods. It explicitly tests stable controls, event timing, phenology, and differential persistence/recovery signals in both models; its feature screen selects decay by year three. A clear sufficiently large difference for Q03 could not be written after this check. Novelty must be 1, rather than claiming recovery analysis was unstudied.
3. Kennedy, Yang & Cohen (2010), *Detecting trends in forest disturbance and recovery using yearly Landsat time series: 1. LandTrendr—Temporal segmentation algorithms*, DOI 10.1016/j.rse.2010.07.008. Bibliographic/classic-method status confirmed in Olofsson et al.'s checked full text; original paper not checked in full and not used as evidence for its results.

Classic design: before–after/control–impact, with Underwood (1994), *On Beyond BACI: Sampling Designs that Might Reliably Detect Environmental Disturbances*, a bibliographic lead, not a checked result. Terms also searched: disturbance memory, spectral recovery, reference-condition return, temporal support, annual embedding change detection. This candidate is now a likely rejection on prior overlap and lack of direct biological recovery ground truth.

## Q04 — crop-diversity change without annual relabeling

Three closest works:
1. Lisaius, Keshav, Blake & Atzberger (2026), *Embedding-based Crop Type Classification in the Groundnut Basin of Senegal*, arXiv:2601.16900. Full text: sources/senegal-crop-2026.pdf/.txt, §§3, 4.7–4.10 (including cross-year classification Table 13), discussion. They evaluate performance, plausibility, transferability and accessibility with TESSERA/AlphaEarth and conventional spectral metrics. Proposed difference: directly quantify error in landscape crop-diversity *change*, separate within-year mapping from frozen-head temporal transfer, and compare corrections under an explicit reference-label budget. Cross-year accuracy itself is not new.
2. Olofsson et al. (2014), *Good practices for estimating area and assessing accuracy of land change*, DOI 10.1016/j.rse.2014.02.015. Full text: sources/olofsson-2014.pdf/.txt, §§2.1–2.2, 3.2–3.3, 4–5; recommendations and worked area-estimation example. Map pixel counts can be biased and probability-based reference sampling/area estimation are established. Proposed difference: whether the new fixed annual representations preserve the direction of a nonlinear crop-diversity trend when yearly labels are absent, and which limited label design repairs it. Do not claim new survey estimators.
3. Feng et al. (2025 preprint; later CVPR 2026), *TESSERA: Temporal Embeddings of Surface Spectra for Earth Representation and Analysis*, arXiv:2506.20380. Full text: sources/tessera-2025.pdf/.txt; the downloaded unversioned PDF is a later revision, while the checked web v3 is explicitly July 2025. §§2.1 and crop appendices compare efficient downstream classification, not calibrated aggregate diversity-change estimates. Proposed difference: the monitoring estimand and budgeted temporal correction, rather than a new representation or model ranking.

Additional current sources: *Mapping complex cropping patterns in China (2018–2021) at 10 m resolution: a data-driven framework based on multi-product integration and Google satellite embedding* (ESSD 2026, DOI 10.5194/essd-18-5601-2026), checked relevant mapping/method/result passages and saved full PDF; *Assessing the generalization of geospatial embeddings for temporal transfer in land cover classification* (SSRN 6890917, 2026), abstract only; *From Foundation Embeddings to Cropland Maps: Label Efficiency, Temporal Transferability and Independent Human Validation* (arXiv:2609.17138), abstract only, binary cultivated/noncultivated. *Innovations through crop switching happen on the diverse margins of US agriculture* (2024) is a substantive crop-diversity lead, including discussion of CDL classification uncertainty; full paper not yet checked. Official NASS FAQ confirms public-domain reuse and warns that CDL is a classified map, not farmer reports; do not call it error-free ground truth.

Terms searched: temporal transfer, label shift, prior probability shift, area-adjusted change, postclassification comparison, crop switching, crop rotation, Shannon crop diversity, effective number of crop types. The narrower contrast is not found within this search range; closely related transfer and error-adjustment studies certainly exist. The new embedding product is necessary to test its promised fixed-representation reuse, but the general monitoring problem predates it. Generalize only if the mechanism/comparison survives multiple representations and correction methods.

## Q05 — intermittent lead-time demand and dependence repair

Three closest works:
1. Willemain, Smart & Schwarz (2004), *A new approach to forecasting intermittent demand for service parts inventories*, DOI 10.1016/S0169-2070(03)00013-X. Full text: sources/willemain-2004.pdf/.txt, §§3–6, especially Markov zero/nonzero bootstrap, summed lead-time distribution, and pooled probability-integral-transform evaluation. It already shows the importance of lead-time distributions and offers a strong dependence-aware comparator. Proposed difference: whether immediate pretrained trajectories avoid or reproduce these issues; no claim that dependence or lead-time distribution validation is newly identified.
2. Ansari et al. (2024), *Chronos: Learning the Language of Time Series*, arXiv:2403.07815, TMLR October 2024. Full text: sources/chronos-2024.pdf/.txt, §§3.1–3.3, 5.1–5.7. Autoregressive sample paths, data scaling, quantization, marginal WQL/MASE evaluation and training-corpus overlap are explicit. Proposed difference: matched-marginal temporal reordering and reserve cost, not standard average forecast accuracy.
3. Kim & Song (2026), *When Time-Series Foundation Models Work for Quick-Commerce Demand Forecasting*, DOI 10.13106/jidb.2026.vol17.no3.89. Full text: sources/quick-commerce-2026.pdf/.txt, §§3–4. It compares foundation/statistical/intermittent methods with 12 monthly origins, demand-pattern strata, and operational bias interpretation. Proposed difference: joint lead-time distributions and controlled dependence-only intervention. Its private data cannot be substituted with an assertion about food banks.

Classic method: Croston (1972), *Forecasting and Stock Control for Intermittent Demands*, confirmed bibliographically and described in the checked Willemain paper; original full text not checked. Terms searched: lead-time demand, cumulative demand distribution, temporal aggregation, inventory calibration, stochastic demand, newsvendor, copula coupling, censored sales. The proposed question has a small difference from a large established literature, and the foundation model is not required to discover the general issue. Its score reflects this concern.
